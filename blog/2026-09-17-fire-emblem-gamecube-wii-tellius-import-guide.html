Fire Emblem's Tellius Duology: Two Consoles, One Story, and a Region Lock

2026-09-17

Today, 17 September 2026, the newest Fire Emblem arrived: Fire Emblem: Fortune’s Weave (ファイアーエムブレム 万紫千紅), a Nintendo Switch 2 exclusive and the eighteenth mainline entry. It is set in the Dagdan Empire during the Heroic Games — “a six-month combat event … where the winner is granted their heart’s desire” — and rather than following one lord, it lets you pick among four Flame Lords and play their separate campaigns.

Release: Nintendo Japan, ファイアーエムブレム 万紫千紅 official site

That structure is not new to the series, and the place it came from is the reason for this article. Nearly twenty years ago Fire Emblem split a campaign between different leaders and handed the game back and forth between them — on a Wii disc that was itself the second half of a story begun on the GameCube. Path of Radiance arrived in 2005, Radiant Dawn in 2007, and between them they tell one continuous story across two consoles: the only time the series has ever done that.

They are also the two entries where importing gets genuinely complicated. The GBA cartridges we covered previously are region-free: a Japanese cart boots on any GBA. Discs are not. Both of these games came out in English, so nobody needs the Japanese disc to play them — and the Japanese disc will not start on a Western console anyway. What the Japanese versions offer is something else, and in the case of Radiant Dawn it is a measurably different game.

  • Fire Emblem: Path of Radiance (ファイアーエムブレム 蒼炎の軌跡) · Nintendo GameCube · 20 April 2005 · Intelligent Systems / Nintendo · product code DL-DOL-GFEJ-JPN · released in North America on 17 October 2005 and Europe on 15 November 2005
  • Fire Emblem: Radiant Dawn (ファイアーエムブレム 暁の女神) · Wii · 22 February 2007 · Intelligent Systems / Nintendo · product code RVL-P-RFEJ · released in North America on 5 November 2007 and Europe on 14 March 2008
  • Radiant Dawn is a direct sequel set three years after Path of Radiance, and a finished GameCube save file carries into it
  • Both consoles are region-locked. A Japanese disc needs a Japanese console — and a Japanese Wii with GameCube ports covers both games at once
  • The Japanese and Western versions of Radiant Dawn are not the same build: Japanese Wikipedia lists added scenes, added weapons and changed skills in the overseas release, and richer dialogue in the Japanese one

Why did Fire Emblem go back to a home console?

Because the GBA games sold. Japanese Wikipedia’s note on the decision is blunt about it: the three Game Boy Advance entries starting with The Binding Blade found a level of success abroad, and that is what put the series back on a television. Path of Radiance was the ninth main entry and, as English Wikipedia puts it, “the first home console entry in the series since Fire Emblem: Thracia 776” — a gap of six years.

Nintendo shipped it in Japan on 20 April 2005, which was Fire Emblem’s fifteenth anniversary to the day. The GameCube disc bought the series its first real technical upgrade: 3D battle maps, pre-rendered cutscenes, and voice acting — in the movies only, but a first regardless. It also arrived, in the developers’ own description, near the end of the GameCube’s life, and still helped sell the hardware.

How closely do Path of Radiance and Radiant Dawn connect?

Closer than any other pair in the series. Radiant Dawn is the tenth entry and a direct sequel, opening three years after the war that ends the first game, on the same continent of Tellius. The connection is mechanical as well as narrative: a completed Path of Radiance save on a GameCube memory card transfers into Radiant Dawn, carrying stat bonuses and unlocking support conversations.

What it does not do is hand you the same army. Radiant Dawn is built in four parts: Micaiah and the Dawn Brigade lead the first, Elincia the second, and Ike the third — so the protagonist you spent the GameCube game raising does not appear for roughly half the running time, and the fourth part brings Micaiah and Ike together. Play them in order, with the transfer, and you get the version of the story the two games were designed to tell. Play Radiant Dawn alone and you start as a stranger in an occupied country, which is also the point.

It was the end of an era in a way nobody knew at the time. English Wikipedia: Radiant Dawn “would be the last mainline Fire Emblem title released for a Nintendo home console until the release of Fire Emblem: Three Houses for the Nintendo Switch in 2019.”

What is different about the Japanese version?

For Path of Radiance, mostly names. The English release renamed several characters — ティアマト became Titania, セネリオ became Soren, ワユ became Mia, ルキノ became Lucia — so a Japanese disc is not simply the English game with Japanese text. Japanese Wikipedia also records one balance change abroad: the Physic staff went from 30 uses to 40.

For Radiant Dawn the gap is much wider, and it runs in both directions. Japanese Wikipedia devotes a section to it, and these are its claims, not ours — the article carries an unsourced-content notice, so treat the list as a guide to what to check rather than as settled fact:

  • The overseas release added conversations and illustrations in Parts 3 and 4, with substantial changes around Micaiah’s dialogue
  • It added unique weapons for Nolan, Edward and Leonardo, with base conversations to go with them, and raised Edward’s and Leonardo’s starting stats
  • It removed the Master Crown requirement for promotion, cutting the number of Master Crowns from thirteen to five
  • It changed two skills outright, and added a gallery, a 16:9 option and, in Europe, five languages
  • The Japanese version has more text: dialogue expands on Hard and above, while the overseas version uses the Normal-mode script at every difficulty

Difficulty labels moved too, which matters if you read English guides while playing a Japanese disc: the Japanese game offers Normal, Hard and Maniac, with Maniac locked until you clear Hard, and the mode the Western version calls Hard is the Japanese Maniac.

Will a Japanese disc run on your console?

No, not on a Western one. Both the GameCube and the Wii are region-locked. A Japanese Path of Radiance disc will not boot on a North American or European GameCube, and a Japanese Radiant Dawn will not boot on a Western Wii. This is the opposite of the GBA situation, and it is the single thing to settle before you buy.

The practical answer is one console for both games. Japanese Wii hardware is the original model, which is backward-compatible with GameCube software, controllers and memory cards — so a Japanese Wii runs the Japanese Wii disc, runs the Japanese GameCube disc, and has the memory card slot the save transfer needs. Nintendo’s later cost-reduced Wii (model RVL-101) deleted the GameCube ports and the memory card slots; it was sold in North America, Europe and Australia. If the console in front of you has four controller ports and two memory card doors on top, it is the one you want.

Region-free workarounds exist — Datel’s FreeLoader and Action Replay discs, or hardware modification — but they are outside what we can vouch for here.

Both Japanese discs are in Japanese only.

How do you find the right Japanese copy of each game?

Each shop indexes these two games differently, and one of them does not index the Wii game’s code at all. We measured this on 17 September 2026; stock changes daily.

  • Path of Radiance at Suruga-ya: search the code, without the suffix. DOL-GFEJ returned four listings in stock and twelve including sold-out ones, all of them this game. The full DOL-GFEJ-JPN returned nothing — Suruga-ya does not index the -JPN suffix
  • Radiant Dawn at Suruga-ya: search the Japanese title. RVL-RFEJ, RVL-P-RFEJ and RFEJ all returned zero. Suruga-ya does index Wii codes — the same search finds other Wii games by code — it simply has no code on this listing. ファイアーエムブレム 暁の女神 Wii returned one copy in stock and eight including sold-out ones
  • On Buyee, use the Japanese title plus the console name for both. For Path of Radiance, ファイアーエムブレム 蒼炎の軌跡 ゲームキューブ returned 142 listings and 92 of the first hundred were the game itself; dropping ゲームキューブ raised the count to 203 and dropped the hit rate to 60%, because strategy guides, soundtrack CDs and Fire Emblem Cipher cards flood in. For Radiant Dawn, ファイアーエムブレム 暁の女神 Wii returned 84 listings, about three in five of them the game
  • Never search Buyee for RFEJ on its own. It returns seventeen listings, fifteen of which are Honda Odyssey oxygen sensors and key fobs — part numbers like 36532-RFE-J01. The full RVL-RFEJ returns one listing, and it is the right one
  • On eBay, filter by region. Both games kept their English titles in the West, so an English-title search lands on Western copies by default. The links below apply eBay’s NTSC-J (Japan) region filter
  • On Kataban, Path of Radiance answers to its code; Radiant Dawn is catalogued as RVL-P-RFEJ, so the -JPN form finds nothing — use the code above or the Japanese title

Two phrases to read in Japanese listings: 箱説なし means no box and no manual, and ディスクのみ / ゲームディスクのみ means the disc on its own. For a GameCube game, 外装スリーブ欠品 means the paper sleeve that goes around the case is missing — the game and case are there.

Tellius is the one Fire Emblem story that needs two machines to finish, and in Japan it needs two discs that both stay in the same locked region. If you have the console for it, the GameCube save card is worth the detour — the sequel was built expecting it.

For where the series was before this, see the GBA trilogy; for where it went next, the DS and 3DS remakes. And for where it has arrived, Fortune’s Weave is out today on Switch 2, handing four campaigns to four lords — a shape this pair of discs was already reaching for, on hardware you now have to hunt for.
