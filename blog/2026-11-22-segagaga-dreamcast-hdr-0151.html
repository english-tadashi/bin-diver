Segagaga on Dreamcast: Sega’s 2001 Game About Saving Sega, Its Codes HDR-0151 and HDR-0083, and Why It Never Got a Second Part

2026-11-22

  • Dreamcast
  • RPG
  • 2000s
  • Sega

Segagaga — セガガガ, printed on the disc as SGGG — is a role-playing simulation game that Sega released in Japan on 29 March 2001 for the Dreamcast. You play a new Sega employee in the year 2025, when Sega holds 3% of the console market and a rival called DOGMA the other 97%, and you have to win the company back by persuading its own developers to make games. It came out two days before Sega stopped making the Dreamcast, at first only through Sega’s mail-order shop, in two editions: a standard one, HDR-0151, at 5,800円, and a limited one, HDR-0083, at 9,800円.

This is the third entry in a series about Dreamcast games that never became a series. There was never a Segagaga 2. One of its stages, a shooting game inside the game, was later released on its own for Japanese mobile phones — a piece of Segagaga taken out, not a second part.

  • Japanese title: セガガガ (disc: SGGG, with the subtitle SEGA SIMULATION), published by Sega, developed by Hitmaker
  • Four editions, three codes: standard HDR-0151 and limited HDR-0083 (29 March 2001, mail order only); shop edition HDR-0171 (31 May 2001); and a budget Dreamcast Collection edition with no code in our sources
  • Japan only: no official release abroad; an English fan translation appeared in 2026
  • No sequel: the shooting stage became the mobile game Segagaga R-720 in 2005, and that is all
  • Buying it: the title at Suruga-ya and on Buyee, which finds every edition; each code finds one

What is Segagaga, and how does it play?

Japanese Wikipedia splits it into two halves. In the role-playing part you go into Sega’s development studios, turned into dungeons, and “persuade” the staff you meet — a battle fought with insults and bluffs rather than weapons. In the simulation part you put the developers you have won over into teams and manage their games to release, from morale to the advertising budget. The goal is 100% of the market. Both Wikipedias stress the in-jokes: old Sega games and characters everywhere, and DOGMA as a parody of Sony. English Wikipedia says it sold about 18,000 copies in its first week and a little over 34,000 in total, and that critics praised the humour.

English Wikipedia says the director and designer, Tez Okano, worked on it in secret for two years before showing it to Sega; the management first took it for a joke, and Hitmaker’s president Hisao Oguchi let it continue on a second presentation. Okano had a marketing budget of $200, half of which went on a promotional wrestling mask. Toei Animation made the animated scenes. These are the sources’ descriptions; we have not played through the game to check them.

Which edition is which?

The Media Arts Database has four rows. The standard edition is HDR-0151 (row M719164, titled SGGG from the disc label, 29 March 2001, [5,800円（税抜）], barcode 4974365501518). The limited edition is HDR-0083 (row M750386, same day, 9800円 (税抜)), packed in a DVD-style case with a Segagaga T-shirt, a set of Sega memorial pins and a notebook. The shop edition is HDR-0171 (row M750387, 31 May 2001, 5800円 (税抜), in a CD case): Japanese Wikipedia says the game was sold only through the Dreamcast Direct mail-order service at first and reached ordinary shops on that date. The fourth row, M750388, is a Dreamcast Collection edition dated 20 March 2003 at 2800円 (税抜), with no code.

Sega’s own Dreamcast list has a single row, 3月29日｜セガガガ｜シミュレーション｜5,800円｜HDR-0083 — the standard price with the limited edition’s code. Suruga-ya keeps them apart by name: セガガガ [通常版] (ドリームキャストダイレクト専売) (standard, mail order only), セガガガ[初回限定](ドリームキャストダイレクト専売) (limited) and セガガガ[店頭版] (shop edition). A サンプル (sample) copy and the SGGG VM memory card come up in the same searches; the memory card, shaped like a Mega Drive by English Wikipedia, is not the game.

English Wikipedia adds one change between printings: after an association objected to a character named “Adult Children”, Sega apologised and reissued the game with the name replaced. Neither source ties that change to a particular code, so we do not either.

Is Segagaga R-720 a sequel?

We do not count it as one. English Wikipedia says the shoot ’em up stage inside Segagaga, inspired by Thunder Force, was released on 2 June 2005 as a standalone mobile phone game for the Sega Ages service, Segagaga R-720, with additional enemies and bosses, such as a giant Sega Saturn. It is one stage of the Dreamcast game taken out and expanded, not a continuation of its story or its company-management game. The soundtrack albums, released several times from 2001 on, are music, not games.

Why was there never a Segagaga 2?

We could not find a reason given anywhere, so we will not offer one. English Wikipedia says the game was not a commercial success, likely because of its late release, and that it came out two days before the Dreamcast was discontinued on 31 March 2001. Neither Wikipedia mentions a sequel.

Has it been re-released?

Only as the budget Dreamcast Collection edition that the Media Arts Database lists for 2003; neither Wikipedia mentions a port. English Wikipedia says an English fan translation was released in 2026, criticised for its use of AI; that is a fan patch, not a release.

Why search Kataban for this one?

Because the editions have different codes and the shops spell the title in more than one way. On Kataban, HDR-0151, HDR-0083 and HDR-0171 each return their own edition; セガガガ and Segagaga return four rows — the three 2001 editions and the 2003 Dreamcast Collection — and SGGG, the title on the disc, returns the standard edition. In second-hand game shops in Japan, such as Book-Off, games are often shelved in Japanese syllabary order, and this one is under セ.

A game about saving Sega, released two days before Sega gave up the console, with one of its stages later sent to phones: this disc was never followed by a second one. If you want the Japanese original, the standard edition is HDR-0151, and the box with the T-shirt is HDR-0083.
