Burning Rangers on Sega Saturn: Sonic Team’s 1998 Firefighting Game, Its Code GS-9174, and Why It Never Got a Second Part

2026-11-12

  • Sega Saturn
  • Action
  • 1990s
  • Sega

Burning Rangers — バーニングレンジャー, printed on the disc as BURNING RANGERS — is a 3D action game that Sega released in Japan on 26 February 1998 for the Sega Saturn, product code GS-9174, at 5,800円 before tax. It was made by Sonic Team. You play a firefighter of the future, sent into burning buildings to put out fires and rescue the people trapped inside, guided not by a map but by a voice on the radio.

This is the seventh and last entry in a series about Sega Saturn games that never became a series. Burning Rangers was one of the last Saturn games released in North America, and there was never a Burning Rangers 2. Some of its ideas, and its theme song, turned up later in other Sonic Team games. At the end of this guide we look back at all seven Saturn games in the series.

  • Japanese title: バーニングレンジャー (disc: BURNING RANGERS), published by Sega, developed by Sonic Team
  • Sega Saturn CD: 26 February 1998 · 5,800円 before tax · GS-9174 — the Media Arts Database and Sega’s own Saturn software list agree on all three
  • In the box: the game disc and a separate music CD with the theme song
  • Western releases: North America and Europe in 1998, among the last Saturn games released there
  • No sequel: its four-player online idea was taken up again in Phantasy Star Online, and its songs were reused in that series, but nothing continued the game itself
  • Buying it: the code at Suruga-ya, and the title plus セガサターン on Buyee — where many listings are demo discs

What is Burning Rangers, and how does it play?

You choose one of two new recruits, Shou or Tillis — both Wikipedias name them — and go into a building on fire. You put out the flames, rescue the people trapped inside and reach the cause of the disaster. English Wikipedia describes the core loop: extinguished fires leave energy crystals, and you need crystals to send each rescued person to safety. Because there is no map, a team member talks you through the corridors over the radio; both Wikipedias call this the voice navigation system, and Japanese Wikipedia says it was added to stop players getting lost, a common complaint about 3D games at the time.

English Wikipedia says the development team was directed by Naoto Ohshima, with Yuji Naka as producer, and that the idea was to make a game about saving people rather than killing them; Japanese Wikipedia names the same two and says the team chose firefighters as a hero many people admire as children. English Wikipedia counts four missions. Japanese Wikipedia says Famitsu gave it 32 out of 40. These are the sources’ descriptions; we have not played through the game to check them.

What is in the box?

The Media Arts Database lists the contents of the Japanese release as one game disc plus a music CD, two flyers and a survey postcard. Suruga-ya’s listings confirm the CD in their own way: several copies are marked テーマソングCD欠品 (“theme-song CD missing”) or note the condition of the テーマソングディスク. So a complete copy has two discs. On Buyee the CD is often called ミニCD or 特典CD, and on eBay “bonus mini CD”, “music CD” or “theme song CD”. If it matters to you, check the photos.

The other thing to filter out is the demo. Suruga-ya lists バーニングレンジャーズ[体験版], and on Buyee and eBay the trial disc appears again and again — on its own, as a magazine giveaway, or paired with The House of the Dead. 体験版, 非売品 (“not for sale”), “trial” or “demo” means it is not the game.

Was it released outside Japan?

Yes, a few months later. Wikipedia’s list of Saturn games dates the North American release to 28 May 1998, citing Sega’s press release, and the European one to 19 June 1998. English Wikipedia says it was among the final five Saturn games released in America and that Sega had planned it to be the last. On eBay the Western and Japanese copies share the name; a listing with “Japan”, “NTSC-J”, “spine”, a bonus CD or GS-9174 is the Japanese one.

Why was there never a Burning Rangers 2?

We could not find a reason given anywhere, so we will not offer one. What we can set out is what exists. The Media Arts Database has one Burning Rangers row, and neither Wikipedia mentions a sequel or a later version.

Two things carried on in other games, and we give them as the sources do. English Wikipedia says that, according to the lead designer Takao Miyoshi, Burning Rangers was first conceived as an online game for four players, became single-player because of network problems, and that Sonic Team took the concept up again in Phantasy Star Online (2000). Japanese Wikipedia lists the Phantasy Star games that reused its songs, from Phantasy Star Online’s quest music to a cover of the theme song in Phantasy Star Online 2. A concept and a song in a different series are not a second Burning Rangers, and for this series they do not count as one.

Has it been re-released?

Not that we could find. Sega’s own Saturn software list links the Burning Rangers row only to its title page, not to a later version, and neither Wikipedia mentions one. The 1998 disc is still the way to play it.

Because the shops mix the game with its demo, and Kataban does not. In second-hand game shops in Japan, such as Book-Off, games are often shelved in Japanese syllabary order, and this one is under ハ.

What did the seven Saturn games have in common?

Each of the seven came close to a second part in its own way, and none got one:

  • Magic Knight Rayearth (GS-9058, 1995) — the anime had a second season; the Saturn game did not
  • Thor (GS-9053, 1996) — itself the follow-up to a Mega Drive game, and the line stopped there
  • Fighters Megamix (GS-9126, 1996) — built from two series, with no second mix
  • Silhouette Mirage (T-32901G, 1997) — its central idea reappeared in Ikaruga
  • Princess Crown (T-14418G, 1997) — a game first planned as its sequel came out as Odin Sphere
  • Machi (T-34001G, 1998) — 428 went back to Shibuya, made as something new
  • Burning Rangers (GS-9174, 1998) — its online idea went into Phantasy Star Online

Ports, reissues, borrowed ideas and spiritual successors — but none of the seven was followed by a second part of its own. That is the Saturn edition of this series. From 13 November it moves back a generation, to games on the Mega Drive that never became series either.
