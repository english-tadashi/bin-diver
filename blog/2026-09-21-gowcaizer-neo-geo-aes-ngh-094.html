Voltage Fighter Gowcaizer on Neo Geo AES: Technōs Japan’s Last Fighter and the Sequel That Never Came

2026-09-21

Voltage Fighter Gowcaizer is a superhero fighting game that Technōs Japan — the studio behind Double Dragon and Kunio-kun — put in arcades on 18 September 1995, with character designs by the animator Masami Ōbari. A month later it came home. On 20 October 1995 it went on sale as a Neo Geo AES cartridge, product code NGH-094, at 29,800円 before tax, under the Japanese title 超人学園ゴウカイザー — “Superhuman Academy Gowcaizer”.

Within a year Technōs Japan was bankrupt, and no second Gowcaizer game was ever made. What the game did get was everything else: a three-episode OVA, a manga, drama CDs, an image song album. Those are adaptations, not sequels, and confusing the two is the fastest way to buy the wrong thing — a search for the Japanese title today returns far more animation cels than cartridges. This guide covers what the cartridge is, why the series stopped, what the OVA actually is, and how the code NGH-094 gets you the cartridge and nothing else.

  • Japanese title: 超人学園ゴウカイザー (Chōjin Gakuen Gōkaizā), developed by Technōs Japan (テクノスジャパン); the arcade and AES releases were published by SNK
  • Neo Geo AES (home cartridge): 20 October 1995 · product code NGH-094 · 29,800円 before tax, as recorded by the Media Arts Database
  • Arcade (Neo Geo MVS): 18 September 1995 · board number NGM-094
  • Also on disc: Neo Geo CD, 24 November 1995, code NGCD-094, published by Technōs Japan itself — the same number, a different format
  • Not a sequel: the 1996–97 OVA, the Tokuma Shoten manga and the drama CDs are adaptations of this one game
  • Still playable: the PlayStation port (17 July 1997) returned as a PlayStation Store download on 14 September 2011, published by Hamster at 617円 including tax

What is Gowcaizer, and who made it?

It is a one-on-one fighting game set in the year 2010, in which students at a private academy transform into armoured “Gowcaizers” using Kaiser Stones. The English Wikipedia article calls it “a 1995 superhero-themed fighting game developed by Technōs Japan and published by SNK for the Neo Geo MVS arcade system, AES home console and Neo Geo CD”, and notes that it was “the company’s second attempt at a fighting game, following the Neo Geo fighting game version of Double Dragon”.

Its selling point was its look. Character designs came from Masami Ōbari, “who previously worked on the anime adaptations of the Fatal Fury series” — which is why the cast looks like a mid-nineties OVA rather than a Neo Geo fighter. Mechanically the game let you pick up your opponent’s special moves, a feature the German magazine MAN!AC singled out even while criticising the animation.

Reviewers were not kind. The scores gathered on that Wikipedia page run from 2 out of 5 in AllGame and Next Generation to 87/100 in Spain’s Hobby Consolas for the Neo Geo CD version, and the article records that the game “was met with generally mixed reception from critics since its release”. In Japan the arcade version charted anyway: Game Machine listed it as the eighteenth most popular arcade game in its 1 November 1995 issue.

Which release is the Japanese cartridge, and what is its code?

Neo Geo releases carry two numbers with the same digits: NGM- for the MVS arcade board and NGH- for the AES home cartridge. Here they are NGM-094 and NGH-094, and the Neo Geo CD release keeps the number as NGCD-094. The arcade database arcade-history files the MVS board as “Choujin Gakuen Gowcaizer [Model NGM-094]”, manufacturer Technos Japan Corp., 1995.

The Neo Geo Master List dates both the Japanese AES and the English AES release to 20 October 1995, so a US cartridge exists as well. Kataban’s catalog tracks the Japanese one, row M764438 in the Agency for Cultural Affairs Media Arts Database, where the publisher is given as テクノスジャパン — Technōs Japan, not SNK. SNK published the game; Technōs made it, and Technōs is the publisher named on the Neo Geo CD release.

That database has no product code for the cartridge row, although it does hold NGCD-094 for the disc. We added NGH-094 to the catalog on 16 September 2026 from the Master List and from a Suruga-ya code search that returns this game and nothing else, so the cartridge’s catalog card now shows it.

Why was there never a sequel?

Because the company did not outlive the game by much. The English Wikipedia article on Technōs Japan puts the end plainly: “By 1996, Technōs Japan declared bankruptcy and ceased operations.” The studio had been founded in December 1981; Gowcaizer arrived in its final full year.

The rights did not disappear with it. “A licensing company named Million Co., Ltd was formed to purchase the former intellectual properties of Technōs Japan,” and then, “In June 2015, Arc System Works acquired all intellectual properties of Technōs Japan from Million Co., Ltd.” Arc System Works has revived Double Dragon and Kunio-kun repeatedly since. Gowcaizer has not had a second game under any of those owners — only ports of the first one.

Those ports are worth knowing, because they are the cheap way to play it. The PlayStation version arrived on 17 July 1997 at 5,800円 before tax, and the Media Arts Database records it returning as a PlayStation Store download on 14 September 2011, published by Hamster at 617円 including tax. If you want to play the game, that is the route. If you want the object, it is the cartridge.

Isn’t the OVA a sequel?

No. It is an adaptation of the same story, and it is the main reason searches for this title go sideways. A three-episode OVA was directed by Masami Ōbari — the game’s own character designer — animated by J.C.Staff and released between 1996 and 1997. The Media Arts Database lists the video releases, the first a 45-minute VHS volume dated 30 August 1996 at 15,180円, with Ōbari credited. In the West, U.S. Manga Corps cut the episodes into an hour-and-a-half feature called Voltage Fighters: Gowcaizer the Movie.

The OVA is not a continuation: it retells and expands the game’s plot, and it contradicts the game in places. Wikipedia notes that in the OVA the hero Kyosuke “is aided by a dog spirit called Kubira instead of a monkey one as in the game”. A manga followed in January 1997 from Tokuma Shoten, drawn by 村正帝 from Ōbari’s concept, along with four drama CDs and an image song collection.

So the shelf of Gowcaizer things is long, and exactly one item on it is a Neo Geo cartridge. Everything else is animation, print or audio spun off a single 1995 fighting game.

Where can you buy the Japanese cartridge today?

Use the code at both shops, but write it differently at each. This is the rare case where the product code works everywhere. At Suruga-ya, drop the hyphen. Both spellings return the same single listing in the default view — the cartridge, sold out at the shop on 20 September 2026, with a list price of ¥32,780 shown and a marketplace line starting at ¥211,750 — but they part company when you ask the shop to include sold-out items: on 18 September 2026 NGH094 returned four listings and all four were this game, while NGH-094 returned eleven of which four were. Suruga-ya splits the code at the hyphen, so the hyphenless form is the precise one. At Buyee either spelling finds it: the same day, both returned the same single auction, titled “SNK NEOGEOソフト NGH-094 超人学園ゴウカイザー【中古】” — one listing, and it is the cartridge.

The title search is where the cels are. On the same day, 超人学園ゴウカイザー on Buyee returned 65 listings, and the first screen was animation cels, drama CDs, a settei art set, an official guidebook and a Neo Geo CD copy. Narrowing to ゴウカイザー ネオジオ returned 16, still mixed: Neo Geo CD discs, an MVS plastic card set, a flyer, even the obi strip from the PlayStation box. The code skips all of it.

Read the format before you pay. ロムカセット or ROM is the AES cartridge; NGCD or ネオジオCD is the 1995 disc; MVS is the arcade board; セル画 is an animation cel; 攻略本 is a guidebook. Prices on both sites are snapshots from the day we looked, not a going rate.

Why search Kataban for this one?

Because the three Japanese releases share a name, and the code separates them. Checked in a desktop browser on 16 September 2026, ゴウカイザー returns three results — the AES cartridge, the Neo Geo CD disc and the PlayStation version — and Voltage Fighter Gowcaizer returns the same three. The fuller Japanese title 超人学園ゴウカイザー returns two, because the Neo Geo CD row is catalogued under the shorter name ゴウカイザー. NGH-094 returns one: the cartridge, with its code, its 1995 date and the kana to look under on a Japanese shelf (チ, for Chōjin).
