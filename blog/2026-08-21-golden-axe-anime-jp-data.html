Golden Axe Is Getting an Anime. Here's What the Original Japanese Releases Actually Look Like

2026-08-21

Paramount+ drops the new Golden Axe animated series on September 16, all 10 episodes at once. If you're reading this, you probably don't need the anime explained to you — you already know where to watch it.

What you might not have is a clear picture of the game it's based on. Sega's 1989 beat-'em-up went through five different regional releases across five platforms, and the differences between them get repeated online with more confidence than accuracy. Before you go hunting for cartridges, here's what's actually true — verified against Sega's own release records, not fan-wiki guesswork.

The release timeline — and one date that surprised us

The arcade version followed the pattern you'd expect: Japan first, the West about four months later.

The Genesis version flips it. The North American cartridge actually shipped one day before the Japanese Mega Drive version — confirmed across three independent sources (The Cutting Room Floor, Sega Wiki, GameFAQs). Sega was pushing for simultaneous worldwide launches on this one, and North America edged ahead by a single day.

Europe, meanwhile, waited almost a full year after Japan. Same console, same game, wildly different rollout depending on which side of the Atlantic you were on.

"The Japanese version is uncensored" — no, it isn't

You'll see this claim repeated in collector forums constantly: Japan got the full game, the West got the watered-down version. It's wrong, and the actual comparison people should be making gets lost because of it.

According to Hardcore Gaming 101 and the Censorship Wiki, the changes made for the Genesis port were applied identically across Japan, North America, and Europe. There is no region-specific censorship on this console. The real comparison isn't "Japanese Genesis vs. Western Genesis" — it's "arcade original vs. Genesis port, period."

What actually changed in the Genesis port, worldwide:

  • The arcade intro (your ally Alex gets killed) was replaced with a narrated text explanation
  • The "Sega Beer Barn" location name was removed
  • Nearly all on-screen blood was cut
  • The ending was simplified

If you're chasing a Japanese Mega Drive cartridge specifically because you heard it has the uncensored arcade content — it doesn't. None of the Genesis/Mega Drive versions do, regardless of region.

Where the real Japan-exclusive content actually is

Three versions genuinely exist only in Japan, and they're worth knowing about if you want something you can't get anywhere else.

PC Engine CD-ROM²

March 1990 · published by Nippon Telenet · product code TJCD0005

This one is faithful to the arcade original — no censorship applied. It uses the CD format for arranged, higher-quality music and adds voiced opening/ending cutscenes for each character. Notably, this wasn't a Sega release; Telenet licensed it. Never released outside Japan.

Master System

1990 · released in the West too — but worth a separate mention

The protagonist is renamed "Tarik" and treated as a distinct character from the arcade's Ax Battler. Single-player only, but it includes all three characters' magic and the full arcade stage lineup. This one did release in North America and Europe, so it's not a Japan-exclusive — but it's a genuinely different cut of the game from either the arcade or the Genesis port.

WonderSwan Color

February 2002 · published by Bandai · product code SWJ-BANC2B

The series' final handheld port. No overseas release, ever.

One more data point worth flagging: Golden Axe III (Mega Drive, 1993) was Japan-exclusive as a physical release. North America only got it in 1995 through Sega Channel, a modem-based download service — no cartridge. The packaged product code G-4093 exists on Japanese carts only.

Bottom line

The anime is already yours to watch. What's harder to get is the PC Engine CD-ROM² version — arcade-faithful, never censored, never left Japan — or a real Golden Axe III cartridge instead of a Sega Channel memory. If the anime brings new attention to the series, Japan-exclusive pressings like these are the ones most likely to move in price. Worth locking in a target now.
