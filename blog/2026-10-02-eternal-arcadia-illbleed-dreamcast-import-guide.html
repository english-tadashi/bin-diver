Eternal Arcadia and Illbleed: Two Japanese Dreamcast Discs, Two Ways to Search

2026-10-02

  • Dreamcast
  • RPG
  • Survival horror
  • 2000s
  • Sega
  • Crazy Games

On 5 October 2000, Sega released エターナルアルカディア (ETERNAL ARCADIA), the Overworks role-playing game that Western players know as Skies of Arcadia. It came on two GD-ROMs, in three Japanese editions on the same day. Less than six months later, on 29 March 2001, Crazy Games — the Blue Stinger team, which had changed its name from Climax Graphics that February — released イルブリード (ILLBLEED), a horror game set in an amusement park built to terrify its visitors.

Both are Japanese discs you can still find in Japanese shops. This guide explains what a Western Dreamcast needs before it will play them, which Eternal Arcadia disc is the full game, and which searches find each: the code HDR-0076 for Eternal Arcadia, the title for ILLBLEED.

Official software lists (Japanese): SEGA — ドリームキャスト software published by Sega · published by licensees

  • Eternal Arcadia: エターナルアルカディア, Sega, 5 October 2000 — HDR-0076 (the LIMITED BOX is HDR-0109, the @barai trial edition HDR-0119)
  • Illbleed: イルブリード, Crazy Games, 29 March 2001 — no code in Kataban’s catalog, so search it by title
  • Region: a Japanese disc will not start on a North American or European Dreamcast without a boot disc or a modchip
  • The right disc: an @barai copy of Eternal Arcadia locks partway through, and Sega suspended sales of the unlock keys on 31 March 2002
  • In the catalog: HDR-0076 finds its row; the Illbleed row is written ILLBLEED, and the katakana title finds nothing

Will a Japanese Dreamcast disc play on your console?

Not on its own. English Wikipedia says the Dreamcast “uses regional lockout, only playing games released within its predetermined region”, and that this “is circumventable via modchip installation, boot discs, or cheat discs such as Datel’s Action Replay”. Import devices were on sale at the time: in 2000 IGN covered the DC-X, a device that “allows you to play Japanese games on American and European consoles”. The Eternal Arcadia box says the same thing from the other side: “For Japan Only”, next to a note that the disc works only on a Japanese Dreamcast marked NTSC J.

The name is the quickest tell. The Japanese box says ETERNAL ARCADIA and carries an HDR- code; the Western ones say Skies of Arcadia, with the code 51052 in North America and MK-51052-50 in Europe. ILLBLEED kept its name in North America, so for that game you have to read the listing.

ETERNAL ARCADIA (HDR-0076): three editions on one day

Sega published the game and Overworks developed it. It reached North America in November 2000 and Europe in April 2001 as Skies of Arcadia, and the Western versions were edited: Hardcore Gaming 101 says the American one “was slightly censored to remove references to smoking and alcohol”. There is little speech to worry about in either language — HG101 notes “almost no voice acting”, beyond short exclamations in battles and story scenes.

In Japan, three editions went on sale on 5 October 2000, and Sega’s product page gives each its own code: the regular edition, HDR-0076; the LIMITED BOX, HDR-0109, which adds a visual book, a mascot strap, a skull bracelet and a blue sky-pirate flag bandana; and the @barai edition, HDR-0119. Sega’s later history list gives HDR-0076 to the @barai edition as well, but the product page and the @barai box itself both print HDR-0119.

The @barai edition is the one to avoid if you want to play to the end. Sega’s page describes it as a trial with a lock partway through the game (通常版のゲームの途中にロックをかけたお試し版): to carry on, you bought an @barai key online. Game Watch calls it the first attempt of its kind for Japanese console software, and Weekly Famitsu gave the disc away — RPGFan reported that readers of issue 618 “will get the @barai version of the game for free”. Sega suspended new key sales, and key re-downloads, on 31 March 2002. At Suruga-ya these discs are labelled ＠barai版 or ファミ通付録体験版; the regular edition is 通常版.

Two years later, on 26 December 2002, Sega brought the game to the GameCube as エターナルアルカディア レジェンド, which Sega’s page calls a complete edition (完全版). It is a different disc for a different console, and a shop search for エターナルアルカディア on its own mixes the two.

ILLBLEED: a horror theme park from the Blue Stinger team

ILLBLEED came from the studio behind Blue Stinger. Game Developer writes that “two Sega Dreamcast games, Blue Stinger and Illbleed would be created” there, and that “Climax Graphics then changed its company name to Crazy Games in February of 2001”. Shinya Nishigaki, in Hardcore Gaming 101’s words, “personally wrote and produced both of Crazy Games’ Dreamcast titles”. Sega’s page describes the setting as the worst horror theme park in history, built only to plunge its visitors into the depths of fear (来場者を恐怖のどん底に叩き込むだけの目的で造った史上最悪のホラー・テーマパーク). You sense what is ahead with a “four-sense monitor”: “Sight, Hearing, Smell and Sixth Sense”, as Game Developer lists them.

Who published it in Japan depends on where you look. Sega’s own product page presents it as a Crazy Games release, with Crazy Games as the contact (お問い合わせ:（株）クレイジーゲーム) and a Sega copyright line, and Sega’s history pages list it among the licensee titles rather than Sega’s own. Sega Retro, on the other hand, says it was “first published in Japan by Sega”, and Suruga-ya files it under Sega.

The Japanese disc has English voices with Japanese subtitles (全編英語音声で日本語字幕), as AUTOMATON notes. North America got the game in April 2001 from AIA USA, which IGN’s review called a “startup”; Game Developer says it “would never see a release in Europe”, and that “international sales of Illbleed totaled 50,000 copies”. In Japan, Crazy Games also sold a limited edition through Dreamcast Direct with a figure of the heroine, Eriko Christy, limited to 1,000 (限定1000個).

The Media Arts Database that Kataban’s catalog is built from has no product code for ILLBLEED, so the catalog row has none. The row is also written only in Latin letters: searching ILLBLEED on this site finds it, while イルブリード returns nothing (checked 15 September 2026). Japanese shops use both spellings — Suruga-ya lists it as ILLBLEED( イルブリード ).

Which searches find them?

At Suruga-ya, drop the hyphen from the code. In the games category on 15 September 2026, HDR-0076 returned two listings, Eternal Arcadia and an unrelated Dreamcast game, グラウエンの鳥籠; HDR0076 returned the regular edition alone. エターナルアルカディア ドリームキャスト also finds the LIMITED BOX, the Famitsu @barai disc, a sky-pirate trial disc and a store demo; without ドリームキャスト, the GameCube version comes first. イルブリード ドリームキャスト returned ILLBLEED alone. Stock and prices as of 15 September 2026: the regular edition of Eternal Arcadia was sold out at Suruga-ya itself, with marketplace copies from ¥1,860 (42 used); the LIMITED BOX was on the marketplace from ¥5,100 (23 used); and ILLBLEED was in stock at ¥16,200–¥16,300, with marketplace copies from ¥6,870 (11 used and new).

On Japanese auctions, the code wins for Eternal Arcadia. Through Buyee on 15 September 2026, the search for HDR-0076 showed results: 2 (regular edition: 2). エターナルアルカディア ドリームキャスト (the title plus “Dreamcast”) returned 38 listings, and many of them were not the game itself but @barai discs, store demos, trial discs, magazines, guidebooks and other goods (the breakdown is in the sources). The code’s result is thin, and auctions turn over every day, so try the title if the code comes back empty. For ILLBLEED, イルブリード ドリームキャスト returned 4 listings, of which 4 were this game. Prices as of 15 September 2026: the regular-edition listings were at ¥1,000 and ¥4,100, and the ILLBLEED listings at ¥13,000–¥45,000.

On eBay, search the Japanese name in English letters. Skies of Arcadia dreamcast, the search this site builds from the English title, returned 85 listings (15 September 2026, from Japan), mostly Western copies (the breakdown of the first 60 is in the sources). Eternal Arcadia dreamcast returned 50 listings, of which 28 were Japanese regular editions, 8 LIMITED BOXes, 9 @barai discs, 4 demo or trial discs and 1 sample. the search for HDR-0076 showed results: 8 (Eternal Arcadia: 5; unrelated items: 3), so the eBay link below searches Eternal Arcadia dreamcast. For ILLBLEED, which kept its name in North America, Illbleed dreamcast returned 19 listings, a mix of Japanese and North American copies, and not every listing says which (the breakdown is in the sources).

On Kataban, use the code for Eternal Arcadia and the Latin title for ILLBLEED. HDR-0076 returns its one catalog row, and エターナルアルカディア returns five. ILLBLEED returns its row; イルブリード returns none.

Of the two, ETERNAL ARCADIA is the easy one: search HDR-0076, and if you search by title, check that the listing is not an @barai disc. The regular edition started at ¥1,860 on Suruga-ya’s marketplace. ILLBLEED is harder to find and dearer: Suruga-ya had it at ¥16,200, and Buyee’s listings started at ¥13,000 (prices as of 15 September 2026). Search it in katakana at Japanese shops and in Latin letters on Kataban.
