What Do “欠品” and “欠け” Mean in a Japanese Game Listing? How Listings Say What Is Missing

2026-11-27

This post in our glossary of Japanese used-game terms follows Junk, CIB, Rank B, Unopened and Near-mint and the obi. It covers the words that tell you something is not in the box: 欠品 (keppin) and 欠け (kake).

What they mean

欠品 means a missing item. 欠け comes from the verb “to be missing” or “to chip”, and in listings it nearly always means the same as 欠品. A few related words turn up with them:

  • なし / 無し — without (説明書なし: no manual)
  • のみ — only (ソフトのみ: the game only)
  • 単品 — on its own (ソフト単品: the game on its own, without the extras of a set)
  • 全 — all (特典全欠け: every bonus item missing)

On Suruga-ya these notes sit in brackets after the title, usually after 状態： (condition), for example (状態：説明書欠品).

Where it matters most

Limited editions. A 限定版 or 初回限定版 is mostly worth its extras: an art book, a CD, a figure. When Suruga-ya writes 特典全欠けソフト単品, you are buying the plain game in a limited-edition listing, and the price should match.

Consoles. 付属品欠品 or 本体のみ means no AC adapter, no cables and often no controller. Japanese AC adapters are made for Japan’s 100 V mains, so you may need a suitable power supply anyway.

Small parts. Notes can be very specific: one Suruga-ya listing for a boxed Parasite Eve II collector’s set mentioned a missing perfume bottle and a missing inner case in the same line.

When 欠け means a chip, not a missing part

Read the word in front. 角欠け is a chipped corner, as in one Neo Geo listing that noted the bottom-left corner of its instruction card was chipped. 内ケース欠け, by contrast, is a missing inner case. If the word before 欠け is a part of the item (a corner, an edge), it is damage; if it is a whole item, it is missing.

What a listing will not mention

Suruga-ya does not count obi strips and postcards as part of the item, and its stores say that obi and leaflets included in the box are not part of what you buy. So a missing registration card or obi may not be noted at all; only a handful of game listings mention ハガキ欠品. Our obi article explains how listings mark the obi when it is there.

What buyers should do

  • If you want a complete copy, look for the absence of these words, then check the photos. Our CIB article covers the words for complete.
  • For a limited edition, compare the price with the standard edition. If the extras are gone, the standard edition is often cheaper.
  • When you use a proxy service, copy the condition note word for word, such as 説明書欠品, so nobody has to guess.
