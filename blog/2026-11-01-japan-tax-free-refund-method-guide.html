Tax-Free Shopping in Japan Now Works by Refund: What Changed on 1 November 2026, at the Shop and at the Airport

2026-11-01

Japan changed how tax-free shopping works for visitors on 1 November 2026. Until the end of October, a tax-free shop took the consumption tax off at the register. For anything bought on or after 1 November, you pay the full price, tax included, and the tax comes back to you only after Japanese customs confirms at the airport or seaport that you are taking the goods out of the country. The government calls this the Refund Method. If you are hunting for cartridges and discs at BOOKOFF or any other used-game shop, here is what it means for you.

  • At the register: pay the price with tax included, and show your passport.
  • At the airport: do the tax-free procedure before you check in your bags.
  • Keep every item on the same receipt with you until customs has checked it.

What changed on 1 November 2026?

The National Tax Agency puts the start date plainly: the Refund Method has applied since 1 November 2026 (令和８年11月１日), introduced by the 2025 tax reform. The Japan Tourism Agency explains the switch from the shopper’s side. Under the system that ran until 31 October 2026, eligible visitors were exempted from consumption tax at the time of purchase and paid the tax-exclusive price. For purchases made on or after 1 November 2026, the tax is refunded after customs confirms at departure that the goods are leaving Japan.

The dividing line is the date you bought something, not the date you fly home. A game bought tax-free on 31 October was bought under the old rules; one bought on 1 November or later is under the new ones.

What do you do at the shop?

Three things stay the same, according to the Tourism Agency’s comparison table of the old and new procedures. The minimum is still a purchase of 5,000円 or more before tax, at the same shop on the same day. The shop still checks your passport if you are in Japan on a short-term stay. And the shop still sends a record of the purchase to the tax authorities.

What changes is the price you pay: the shop now sells to you with tax included, and the refund comes later. Two other rules from the old system are gone. The separate cap on consumables and the special sealed packaging for them have been abolished; instead, you can only buy as many items as you can carry out of Japan yourself when you leave. If you use up consumables such as food or cosmetics in Japan, you cannot get the tax back on them. That last point rarely matters for games, but the “carry it out yourself” rule does: the refund depends on you having the items with you at the airport.

What do you do at the airport?

This is the step the old system did not have, and the one that decides whether you get your money back.

  • Within 90 days of buying: when you leave Japan, present your passport at a tax-free procedure terminal (a kiosk or electronic terminal) at the airport or seaport. You must do this within 90 days of the purchase date to receive the refund.
  • Before you check in your bags: the terminals and the customs inspection area are in the international departure lobby, before baggage check-in. You need to have all the goods with you. Once your suitcase has gone to the airline, you cannot do the procedure, and the airline will not hand the bag back for it. Arrive early: the customs check, and an inspection if one is required, has to be finished before you check in for your flight.
  • Green or red: after you present your passport, the terminal shows whether an inspection is needed. If not, you are done. If so, customs inspects the goods at the inspection area.
  • One receipt, all the items: customs checks per purchase transaction, such as a receipt. If even one item from a receipt is missing, none of the items on that receipt can be confirmed for a refund.
  • Connecting flights: if you fly domestically before your international flight, do the procedure at the last airport you leave Japan from.
  • Visit Japan Web: at Narita, Haneda, Kansai, Chubu, Fukuoka, New Chitose and Naha, you can also complete the procedure online through Visit Japan Web, inside the departure-lobby area covered by the dedicated Wi-Fi (up to the security checkpoint).

How is the refund paid?

Not by the government. The refund comes from the shop you bought from, or from a refund service provider the shop has hired. The Tourism Agency says the method differs from shop to shop and lists possible examples: bank transfer, a refund to a credit card, payment through an app, or cash at the airport or seaport after the customs check. Which one applies, and when the money arrives, is something the shop explains when you buy. Ask before you leave the register.

Buying at BOOKOFF and other used-game shops

BOOKOFF’s own tax-free page in English, checked on 15 October 2026, lists the conditions as a purchase of ¥5,000 or more before tax and showing your passport, and says a designated handling fee is charged for tax-free purchases. It did not yet describe the Refund Method or how BOOKOFF pays refunds. Other chains and independent shops will each have their own arrangements, so the only reliable answer is the one you get at the register.

If the person at the register does not speak English, show them this line. It is also on Kataban’s shop-staff screen (see below).

返金はどの方法で受け取れますか？
“How will I receive my refund?”

Do this on the day you buy, not at the airport: the refund method is set up through the shop, and the airport terminal only confirms that the goods are leaving Japan.

Using Kataban’s shop-staff screen

You do not need to read any Japanese for this. Search for any game on Kataban, tap “Show to shop staff”, and show the screen to the person at the register, two questions in this order:

  1. Tap “Tax-free?” The screen asks, in Japanese, whether you can buy tax-free and get the tax refunded when you leave Japan. The staff taps one answer, and it fills the screen in Japanese and English: “Yes — you pay the tax now and get it back when you leave Japan”, “Please show your passport”, “Below the tax-free minimum (¥5,000 before tax, same shop, same day)”, “Not available at this store”, or “When you leave, get it checked at airport customs before you check in your bags”.
  2. Then tap “Refund how?” The screen asks the line above, 返金はどの方法で受け取れますか？ (“How will I receive my refund?”). The staff taps one answer: “Bank transfer”, “Back to your credit card”, “Through an app”, “Cash at the airport when you leave”, or “Please read this guide”.

Tap “Ask again” to go back to the questions. If the answer to the first question is yes, the second one is the one that tells you how your money comes back.
