Princess Crown on Sega Saturn: Atlus’s 1997 Action RPG, Its Codes T-14418G and T-14425G, and Why It Never Got a Second Part

2026-11-10

  • Sega Saturn
  • RPG
  • 1990s
  • Atlus

Princess Crown — プリンセスクラウン — is a side-scrolling action RPG released in Japan on 11 December 1997 for the Sega Saturn, product code T-14418G, at 5,800円 before tax. It was directed by George Kamitani (神谷盛治) and finished at Atlus with Sega’s help. You play Gradriel, a thirteen-year-old queen who slips out of her castle to help her people herself.

This is the fifth entry in a series about Sega Saturn games that never became a series, and the one with the closest thing to a sequel. Kamitani later founded Vanillaware, and its Odin Sphere is called this game’s spiritual successor — and, by Japanese Wikipedia, was first planned as Princess Crown 2. A Dreamcast sequel was planned and cancelled. What never came out was a second Princess Crown. On the Saturn it has two Japanese editions with two codes, the 1997 first edition and a 1998 Saturn Collection reissue.

  • Japanese title: プリンセスクラウン, published by Atlus, made by Atlus and Sega
  • First edition: 11 December 1997 · 5,800円 before tax · T-14418G
  • Saturn Collection reissue: 10 December 1998 · 2,800円 before tax · T-14425G — the same game at a lower price
  • Later versions: a PSP port (2005, reissued in 2007), sold as a download that also runs on the PS Vita, and a PS4 version given away in 2019
  • No second part: Odin Sphere (2007) is described as its spiritual successor; the planned Dreamcast sequel was cancelled
  • Buying it: search the code at Suruga-ya, and the title plus セガサターン on Buyee

What is Princess Crown, and how does it play?

Both Wikipedias describe the same shape. You walk through towns and along roads in two dimensions, and when an enemy appears the fight starts on the same screen, side-on, like a fighting game — Japanese Wikipedia stresses that it needs none of a fighting game’s complicated inputs, and English Wikipedia calls it a beat ’em up-style battle system worked by one attack button. There are four playable characters: Queen Gradriel, the knight Edward, the pirate Portgus and the young witch Proserpina, each with a story of their own. English Wikipedia adds that a fifth scenario opens once all four are finished.

What people remember is how it looks. Japanese Wikipedia credits the characters’ smooth movement to sprites split into many jointed parts, and English Wikipedia says the game was later praised for its art. Japanese Wikipedia also says the story is framed as a grandmother reading a fairy tale to her granddaughter. These are the sources’ descriptions; we have not played through the game to check them.

Which Saturn disc is which: T-14418G or T-14425G?

The first edition is T-14418G. The Media Arts Database has it as row M755665, published on 11 December 1997, brand アトラス, 5800円 (税抜). Sega’s own list of licensed Saturn software gives the same date, price and code, with Atlus as publisher.

The Saturn Collection reissue is T-14425G. The Media Arts Database has it as row M755666, published on 10 December 1998, edition セガサターンコレクション, 2800円 (税抜). Japanese Wikipedia gives the same date for the reissue, Suruga-ya lists it as プリンセスクラウン サタコレシリーズ at 3,080円 (2,800 × 1.1), and the disc database redump lists its serial as T-14425G with the edition “Satakore”.

In a listing, サタコレ or サターンコレクション (“Saturn Collection”), or “Satakore”, “Satacolle” or “SC” in English, means the 1998 reissue; their absence and a 1997 date usually mean the first edition. Suruga-ya happens to spell the first edition with a space, プリンセス クラウン, and the reissue without one. The game on both discs is the same.

One more thing to filter out: the PSP version shares the title. A listing that says PSP, ベスト版 (“best edition”) or UMD is the 2005 port, not a Saturn disc.

How is it related to Odin Sphere and Vanillaware?

The sources describe a line of influence, in their own words, and we give them as they are. English Wikipedia says Princess Crown “continued to influence Kamitani’s career, leading to the founding of his company Vanillaware and the development of the spiritual successor Odin Sphere”. Japanese Wikipedia lists Odin Sphere as a related title and says it was first planned as 『プリンセスクラウン2』. The Media Arts Database records Odin Sphere as a 2007 PlayStation 2 game published by Atlus.

So: planned as a sequel, released as something else, by a different developer. Vanillaware is Kamitani’s own company, founded after Princess Crown; Odin Sphere came out under its own title, and neither source calls the released game Princess Crown 2. For this series that is the line we draw — a developer carrying his ideas to his own studio is not the same as a second part of the game — and we have kept the sources’ words rather than replace them with ours.

The sequel that was planned as one never appeared. English Wikipedia says a Dreamcast sequel was cancelled, and later revived by Vanillaware as Dragon’s Crown. That last point is from English Wikipedia alone.

Has it been re-released?

Yes, but not on the Saturn after 1998. Both Wikipedias say it was ported to the PSP in 2005; Japanese Wikipedia dates the port to 22 September 2005 and its budget reissue in the Atlus Best Collection to 8 March 2007, and the Media Arts Database has PSP rows for 2005 and 2007. Sega’s own Saturn list links the Princess Crown row to a PlayStation Store page, labelled ▶PS Vita（PSPダウンロードソフト）: the PSP version, sold as a download that runs on the PS Vita. Japanese Wikipedia adds that a PS4 version, 『プリンセスクラウン 復刻版』, was given as a first-print bonus with 13 Sentinels: Aegis Rim on 29 November 2019; that detail is from Japanese Wikipedia alone. All of these are the same game, not new ones.

English Wikipedia says the game was released only in Japan, and the Saturn listings we found on eBay were all Japanese copies.

Why search Kataban for this one?

Because one name covers two Saturn discs and three PSP entries, and Kataban keeps them apart. T-14418G returns the first edition and T-14425G the Saturn Collection disc, each with the Japanese title next to it to copy into a shop. Princess Crown or プリンセスクラウン shows all five with their platforms, and Odin Sphere takes you to the games that came after it from Vanillaware. In second-hand game shops in Japan, such as Book-Off, games are often shelved in Japanese syllabary order, and this one is under フ.

A queen who leaves her castle, a director who left to found his own studio, and a second part that turned into a different game: Princess Crown stayed a single game. If you want it as first sold in 1997, the code is T-14418G; the 1998 reissue is T-14425G.
