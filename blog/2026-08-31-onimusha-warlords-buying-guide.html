Onimusha: Warlords — A Buying Guide to the PS2 Original

2026-08-31

Onimusha: Warlords put Capcom's samurai-action series on the map in 2001, and it's still the easiest entry point into the series for collectors outside Japan. Here's what you need to know to find and buy the original PlayStation 2 release.

  • Game: Onimusha: Warlords (鬼武者) — PlayStation 2, 2001
  • Publisher: Capcom
  • Product code: SLPM65010
  • Also available: a 2018 remaster on PS4 and Nintendo Switch (Japan-only listing in our data, no confirmed English packaging)

What is Onimusha: Warlords, and why does it matter?

Onimusha: Warlords is the first entry in Capcom's Onimusha series, and it set the shape everything after it followed: a Sengoku-era setting, a samurai protagonist, and demons to cut through. Mechanically it grew out of the house style Capcom had been refining through the late 1990s — fixed camera angles over pre-rendered backgrounds, deliberate room-to-room movement — but pointed at sword combat rather than survival horror. It arrived on the PlayStation 2 in the same stretch when Capcom was pushing that template toward faster, more action-driven games, the direction that also produced Devil May Cry. The two are frequently discussed together for that reason, though they are separate projects.

For a collector, the practical argument for starting here is simpler than the historical one. It's the first game, so nothing is spoiled and no prior entry is assumed. It was a mainstream PS2 release rather than a limited or late-generation one, which means copies are genuinely common on the Japanese secondhand market instead of being a hunt. And it's one of the entries with the widest name recognition outside Japan, so listings are easy to identify even if you don't read Japanese — the Western title is well established, and the Japanese title is a short, distinctive three-character word. If you want a single disc that explains what the series is, this is the one to buy first.

What is the product code for the original PS2 release?

The product code for the original Japanese PlayStation 2 release is SLPM65010. Japanese PS2 discs carry an SLPM- or SLPS-prefixed code printed on the spine and disc face, and that code is the most reliable thing to search on: it identifies one specific release, where a title alone can pull in sequels, budget reissues and unrelated merchandise. This one works as a search key both inside Kataban and on Suruga-ya, which is not true of every product code.

Where can you buy Onimusha: Warlords today?

The most reliable route is searching by product code on Suruga-ya; Buyee and eBay work best by title search instead. This is an ordinary used PS2 game, not a rarity, and copies circulate steadily on the Japanese secondhand market. Two things are worth checking before you commit. First, completeness: PS2 games shipped in a jewel case with a manual and a spine card, and cheaper listings are often disc-and-case only, so a disc-only copy and a complete one can sit at very different prices for the same game. Second, which pressing you are looking at — alongside the 2001 original there are two later budget reissues of the same game (MEGA HITS! in 2001 and the ベスト版 in 2005), and a title search will show all three. Searching the product code avoids that ambiguity, because it resolves to the original release only.

Note that a 2018 remaster exists on PS4 and Nintendo Switch. In our data it appears only as a Japanese release, and we have not confirmed English packaging for it, so if you specifically want the 2001 original, check the platform on the listing rather than assuming.

Spotting it on the shelf at Bookoff

If you're browsing in person — say, at a Bookoff in Tokyo — you won't see SLPM65010 printed anywhere on the shelf tag. Japanese used-game shops label stock with the release month, a condition grade (here: C), and a tax-included price (税込) — not a product code. What you're looking for is the black case with the 鬼武者 logo in black over an orange flame background, the flame burning out to white behind the strokes, with a red accent stroke and the CAPCOM mark at the bottom right. If the exact copy isn't in stock, staff can sometimes order it from another branch, though that conversation requires Japanese. At Bookoff specifically, expect a used price in the ¥700–800 range (tax included) for a copy in playable condition.
