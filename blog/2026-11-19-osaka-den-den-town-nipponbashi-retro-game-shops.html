Osaka Den Den Town (Nipponbashi): Three Retro Game Shops and How to Use Kataban Inside Them

2026-11-19

Den Den Town, the shopping street in Osaka’s Nipponbashi district, has three shops whose own websites say they sell retro games: Super Potato’s Ota Road branch, Suruga-ya’s Osaka Nipponbashi main store, and Suruga-ya’s Nipponbashi Den Den Town store, which opened on 19 October 2026. Below are their addresses and hours as listed on 7 November 2026, and how to use Kataban on the shop floor: read the code on a cartridge, find the right shelf, ask the staff, and check Suruga-ya’s online shop.

  • Where: Nipponbashi, Naniwa Ward, Osaka. Ebisucho and Nipponbashi stations on the Sakaisuji Line lead into the street.
  • Shops: Super Potato Ota Road, Suruga-ya Osaka Nipponbashi main store, Suruga-ya Nipponbashi Den Den Town. The last one is so new that only Suruga-ya’s own announcement describes it.
  • In the shop: switch Kataban to I’m in Japan, search the title or code, and use Show to shop staff to ask in Japanese.
  • Before you go: hours change. Check each shop’s page on the day.

Where is Den Den Town in Osaka?

Den Den Town is another name for Nipponbashi, in Osaka’s Naniwa Ward, south of Namba. The Osaka Convention & Tourism Bureau describes it as western Japan’s largest electronics and otaku district, with about 150 shops along roughly a kilometre of covered street, and English Wikipedia says it has more recently become known for anime and otaku shopping, like Tokyo’s Akihabara.

Both sources name the same two stations: Ebisucho on the Osaka Metro Sakaisuji Line, and Nipponbashi on the Sakaisuji and Sennichimae lines. The tourism bureau also lists Namba on the Midosuji Line and Nankai Namba, each about five minutes away or less. Shop hours vary, so there is no single opening time for the street.

The Den Den Town store is new. Suruga-ya’s page says it reopened on Monday 19 October under this name, combining two earlier Nipponbashi shops, 駿河屋 日本橋乙女館 and 駿河屋 日本橋オタロードアウトレットおみやげ館, and Suruga-ya’s store list showed the new name on 7 November 2026. We found no independent listing of it yet, so everything in its row comes from Suruga-ya alone.

None of the three pages mentions tax-free shopping. Ask at the counter; Kataban’s staff screen has a question for it.

How do you use Kataban inside a Japanese game shop?

Kataban has an in-shop mode. Tap I’m in Japan at the top of the search page, then search an English title, a Japanese title or a code. We checked each step on the live site on 7 November 2026 with GS-9058, the Sega Saturn Magic Knight Rayearth:

  • The code. The result shows the product code with a copy button, so you can compare it with the spine of a box on the shelf.
  • The shelf. Japanese shops file games by the reading of the title. For Rayearth the result says to look under マ, or the ま行 row where shelves are split by row.
  • The staff. Show to shop staff opens a screen with the title, code and system in Japanese and a question to pick: in stock, glass case, box and manual, testing, tax-free, refunds, or other stores. The staff can tap an answer such as はい、あります (“yes, we have it”) or 在庫はありません (“out of stock”).
  • The barcode. If you have a boxed game in your hand, open the scanner and point it at the barcode on the back.

Each result also says whether the game needs a Japanese console. For Rayearth it notes that Saturn discs are region-locked.

How do you compare the shelf with Suruga-ya’s online shop?

Under Buy this game, the result has Search Suruga-ya.jp by code and by title. They open Suruga-ya’s online shop, so you can see whether it has the game and at what price before you pay at the counter. On 7 November 2026, the code search for Rayearth’s standard edition, GS9058, showed one copy in stock online.

That is Suruga-ya’s mail-order stock, not the shelf in Nipponbashi. To see a single store, use its page on Suruga-ya’s site: both Nipponbashi stores have a link headed 商品在庫検索 (stock search) that opens Suruga-ya.jp limited to that store. Suruga-ya says the prices shown are Suruga-ya.jp prices and that an item may already have sold in the store, so ask the staff for the latest price and stock.

What should you check before you go?

  • Hours on the day. All three pages list hours and say the shops are open every day. The Den Den Town store’s page adds that it may close temporarily and asks you to contact the shop.
  • Which Suruga-ya. Suruga-ya lists several shops in Nipponbashi. The two in the table are the ones whose pages mention games; check the shop name before you walk in.
  • The station. Super Potato’s page gives no nearest station; use the map link.
