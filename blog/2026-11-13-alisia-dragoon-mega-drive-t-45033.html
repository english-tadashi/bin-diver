Alisia Dragoon on Mega Drive: Game Arts’ 1992 Lightning Action Game, Its Code T-45033, and Why It Never Got a Second Part

2026-11-13

  • Mega Drive (Genesis)
  • Platformer
  • 1990s
  • Game Arts

Alisia Dragoon — アリシアドラグーン — is a side-scrolling action game that Game Arts released in Japan on 24 April 1992 for the Mega Drive, product code T-45033, at 7,800円 before tax. You play Alisia, a young sorceress who fires lightning from her hands and fights with one of four pet monsters at her side, on a journey to avenge her father. The anime studio Gainax worked on the art and the story.

This is the first entry in a series about Mega Drive games that never became a series. Alisia Dragoon came out in North America first, published by Sega, and there was never an Alisia Dragoon 2. Its lightning attack goes back to an older Game Arts computer game, Thexder, but nothing was built on Alisia Dragoon itself.

  • Japanese title: アリシアドラグーン (Media Arts Database: アリシア ドラグーン), published by Game Arts, with art and story by Gainax
  • Mega Drive cartridge: 24 April 1992 · 7,800円 before tax · T-45033 — the Media Arts Database and Sega’s list of licensee Mega Drive games agree on the date and the price, and Sega’s list gives the code
  • Western releases: published by Sega, in North America a few weeks before the Japanese release, with very different cover art
  • No sequel: its system came from Thexder (1985), and no later game continued it
  • Re-released: on the Mega Drive Mini in 2019, and later on Nintendo Classics
  • Buying it: the code at Suruga-ya and on Buyee, with the title plus メガドライブ as a second Buyee search

What is Alisia Dragoon, and how does it play?

Both Wikipedias describe the same game: eight side-scrolling levels, each ending with a boss. Alisia’s lightning aims itself at enemies in range, but every volley drains her power and makes the next one weaker; stop firing and it recharges, and at full charge it hits every enemy on the screen. One of her four pets flies around her, attacking enemies and blocking shots, and you can switch between them or fight alone at any time. English Wikipedia names them the Dragon Frye, the Boomerang Lizard, the Thunder Raven and the Ball O’ Fire. There is no save.

The two Wikipedias agree that Game Arts did most of the work and Gainax handled the art and the story; English Wikipedia says several Gainax founders had worked on Hayao Miyazaki’s films, and that some levels show the influence of Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind. The music is by Mecano Associates. Japanese Wikipedia says Famicom Tsūshin gave it 19 out of 40, and readers of Mega Drive Fan 22.69 out of 30. These are the sources’ descriptions; we have not played through the game to check them.

Which release is the Japanese one?

The Japanese cartridge is the one Game Arts published, T-45033. Sega’s own list of Mega Drive games released by licensees has the row 4月24日｜アリシアドラグーン｜ゲームアーツ｜アクション｜7,800円｜T-45033｜８Ｍ, an 8-megabit cartridge. The Media Arts Database row M745087 gives the same date and price, with Game Arts as publisher, and writes the title with a space, アリシア ドラグーン.

Outside Japan the game was published by Sega. Japanese Wikipedia dates the North American release to 30 March 1992, before the Japanese one, and both Wikipedias say Sega changed the cover: the Japanese box shows a slight, anime-style heroine, and the Western box a barbarian warrior — in a golden bikini, as English Wikipedia puts it. That is the quickest way to tell copies apart. Suruga-ya sells both, but files the Western ones by their region: GENESIS(北米)版 ALISIA DRAGOON and MEGADRIVE(EU)版 ALISIA DRAGOON. On eBay, “Genesis”, “PAL” or “USA” means a Western copy, and “Japan”, “NTSC-J” or T-45033 the Japanese one.

Is Alisia Dragoon a sequel to Thexder?

No. Japanese Wikipedia says the game inherits its game system from Thexder, a 1985 Game Arts game for Japanese home computers, and lists Satoshi Uesaka, who made Thexder, as its original concept; it quotes the book メガドライブ大全 (2004) on the lightning, a fully automatic homing attack carried over from Thexder. A borrowed system is not a continuation: Alisia, her world and her story are new, and Thexder had its own sequel, Fire Hawk: Thexder 2, which English Wikipedia mentions as another game Mecano Associates wrote music for.

Why was there never an Alisia Dragoon 2?

We could not find a reason given anywhere, so we will not offer one. What both Wikipedias do say is that it sold few copies in Japan, and English Wikipedia adds that sales outside Japan were weak too, despite good reviews; Japanese Wikipedia says it was not widely promoted. The Media Arts Database has one Alisia Dragoon row, and neither Wikipedia mentions a sequel.

Has it been re-released?

Yes, twice, as the original game. Japanese Wikipedia says it was included on the Mega Drive Mini in 2019, and English Wikipedia says it was later made available on the Genesis Mini and Nintendo Classics. These are the 1992 game on new hardware, not a new version, so for this series they do not count as a second part. They do show up in shop searches: on Buyee, the title search returns Mega Drive Mini consoles alongside the cartridge.

Where can you buy the Japanese cartridge today?

On 17 October 2026 we counted 2 listings of this game among 3 Buyee results for the code, 6 among 12 for the title, and 1 in stock at Suruga-ya; eBay’s search returned 66 results, with Genesis and European copies mixed in.

Why search Kataban for this one?

Because the shops mix the Japanese cartridge with Western copies and Mega Drive Minis, and Kataban does not. T-45033, Alisia Dragoon, アリシアドラグーン and the database’s アリシア ドラグーン each return this cartridge and nothing else, with the Japanese title next to it to copy into a shop. In second-hand game shops in Japan, such as Book-Off, games are often shelved in Japanese syllabary order, and this one is under ア.

A system borrowed from Thexder, a heroine redrawn for the West, and two re-releases of the same game: this cartridge was never followed by a second one. If you want the Japanese original, the code is T-45033, and the box with the anime-style heroine is the one.
