Kaifuuhin, B-hin, Wakeari: What Opened, Second-Grade and “With a Reason” Mean on Japanese Game Listings

2026-11-20

開封品 (kaifūhin) means an opened item, B品 (B-hin) a second-grade or imperfect product, and 訳あり (wakeari, also written ワケあり) an item with some reason it is sold differently, often a flaw. Shops do not use them the same way. Suruga-ya says all its used goods count as opened, BOOKOFF lists four kinds of ワケあり品, and we found no game shop that defines B品 for games at all. Here is what each word means, how it differs from 未開封, 美品 and ジャンク, and how often it actually appears in listings.

  • 開封品: opened. At Suruga-ya, every used item is one.
  • B品: a second-grade product. Rare in game listings; Suruga-ya uses ランクB instead.
  • 訳あり: “with a reason”. At BOOKOFF, often an item the shop cannot guarantee works. On Buyee, mostly bundles of games.
  • Before you buy: ask what the reason is. The word alone does not tell you.

What do 開封品, B品 and 訳あり mean?

JapaneseReadingGeneral meaningWhat the shops say
開封品kaifūhinAn item whose package has been opened (開封, “opening” a package)Suruga-ya: used goods are opened items, because it checks them when buying them in
B品B-hinA second-rate or imperfect product, sold at a discountNo game shop definition found. Suruga-ya uses ランクB
訳あり / ワケありwakeariHaving special circumstances; imperfect merchandiseBOOKOFF: varies by store; mainly old items, items it cannot guarantee work, cheap items sold without a guarantee, and parts such as cables

The general meanings come from the JMdict dictionary, which gives Ｂ品 as “second-rate article; imperfect product (sold at a discounted price)” and 訳あり as “imperfect (merchandise); with defects”. A Japanese fashion-trade glossary describes B品 the same way, as goods that fall short of the regular quality standard, notes that they are also called 訳あり商品, and adds that the standard differs from company to company.

How do Suruga-ya and BOOKOFF use these words?

Suruga-ya. Its policy for used goods says that, because it checks every used item when buying it, its used goods are 開封品. So on Suruga-ya the word does not mark anything special: any used game there has been opened. The same page mentions ワケあり商品 only in a list of items that cannot be returned to the maker, without defining it, and it does not use B品. For a damaged item it uses ランクB, which it treats as a separate product from the normal one.

BOOKOFF. Its Q&A answers what ワケあり品（サービス品） is. It says this differs by store and lists four main cases: the item is old; damage or missing parts mean the store cannot guarantee it works; the original price was so low that it cannot be sold with a guarantee; and parts such as cables or unused consumables. Our guide to buying without reading Japanese lists other words you will see on Japanese listings.

How are they different from 未開封, 美品 and ジャンク?

  • 未開封 (unopened). Suruga-ya: still shrink-wrapped or sealed in a way that cannot be restored once opened. The opposite of 開封品.
  • 美品 (near-mint). Suruga-ya: opened, but with no sign of use, as good as new. An item can be both 開封品 and 美品.
  • ジャンク (junk). Suruga-ya sells items marked junk with no claims and no returns. BOOKOFF’s ワケあり品 includes items it cannot guarantee work, which is close to junk, but BOOKOFF itself says the meaning differs by store.

Each of these has its own guide: ランクB, 未開封品 and 美品, ジャンク, and CIB and 箱説付き. Edition words such as 通常版 and 限定版 are in our guide to Japanese game editions.

How often do these words appear in game listings?

We searched Suruga-ya’s game category and Buyee on 7 November 2026.

  • Suruga-ya splits the search words, so a search does not show how often a word is used. B品 returned 1,003 in-stock items, led by USB cables whose names contain “Type-B”. For 開封品 (97 in-stock items), none of the 24 titles on the first page contained the word. Suruga-ya writes conditions in its own form instead, such as (状態：説明書欠品) (“condition: manual missing”).
  • Buyee (mostly Yahoo! Auctions listings, written by sellers): of the first 100 results for 開封品 ゲームソフト, 51 titles said 未開封品 (unopened) and only two said 開封品 on its own. B品 ゲームソフト returned 41 listings and none had B品 in the title. For 訳あり ゲームソフト, 83 of the first 100 titles used the word, and 54 of those were sets or bulk lots.

So when you see 開封品 in a search result, check that the title does not actually say 未開封品, which means the opposite.

What should you ask before buying an item marked 訳あり?

Ask what the reason is. BOOKOFF’s list shows how different the reasons can be, from an old release to a part with no guarantee. On Buyee, many 訳あり listings are bulk lots, so check the photos for the games you want and read the description for what is missing or untested. If you are in a shop in Japan, Kataban’s Show to shop staff screen has questions for box and manual and for testing.
