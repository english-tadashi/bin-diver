Napple Tale on Dreamcast: Sega’s 2000 Daydream Adventure, Its Code HDR-0079, and Why It Never Got a Second Part

2026-11-21

  • Dreamcast
  • Platformer
  • 2000s
  • Sega

Napple Tale: Arsia in Daydream — ナップルテール — is an action game that Sega released in Japan on 19 October 2000 for the Dreamcast, product code HDR-0079, at 5,800円 before tax. A girl named Porch is carried off by mistake from a summer festival into Napple World, a land of daydreams where spring, summer, autumn and winter are separate places, and has to bring back the six petals that flew out of her to get home. Its music is by Yoko Kanno, with songs by Maaya Sakamoto.

This is the second entry in a series about Dreamcast games that never became a series. There was never a Napple Tale 2. The game came back once, as a cheaper Dreamcast Collection edition with the same code, and never left Japan except through a fan translation.

  • Japanese title: ナップルテール, written Napple Tale Arsia in Daydream on the box; published by Sega
  • Dreamcast GD-ROM: 19 October 2000 · 5,800円 before tax · HDR-0079 — the Media Arts Database and Sega’s own Dreamcast software list agree on all three
  • Budget edition: the Dreamcast Collection release, also HDR-0079, at 2,800円
  • Japan only: an unofficial English patch appeared in 2019
  • No sequel: neither Wikipedia mentions one
  • Buying it: the code at Suruga-ya and on Buyee, with the title plus ドリームキャスト as a second Buyee search

What is Napple Tale, and how does it play?

English Wikipedia describes two halves. Napple Town is a 3D hub, compared with Sonic Adventure, where Porch helps the townspeople with around seventy small problems, from building a house to settling a quarrel. From the town, side-scrolling stages lead into the four seasons, each ending with a boss. Enemies drop the parts of recipes for “Paffets”, creatures that become furniture for the town or helpers in the stages, 71 in all. Japanese Wikipedia gives Sega’s own name for the genre, ラブリーポップ・アクションRPG (“lovely pop action RPG”), and says it was designed for women and casual players, with each scenario about thirty minutes long and the difficulty kept low; English Wikipedia says its creative staff were mostly women.

Both Wikipedias single out the music: Yoko Kanno wrote it — more than 70 tracks, by Japanese Wikipedia — and Maaya Sakamoto sang the opening and ending themes and voiced Porch. Japanese Wikipedia gives Famitsu’s cross review as 29 out of 40. These are the sources’ descriptions; we have not played through the game to check them.

Which edition is which?

Both editions carry the same code, so the code alone will not tell them apart. The Media Arts Database has the original as row M719202, 19 October 2000, [5800円(税抜)], and the Dreamcast Collection edition (ドリコレ) as row M719066, [2800円(税抜)], barcode 4974365502102 — both HDR-0079. The date of the budget edition is split: the database gives 6 March 2003, Japanese Wikipedia 6 March 2002. We give both.

In shops the difference is in the name. Suruga-ya lists the original as ナップルテール -Arsia in Daydream- and the budget one as Napple Tale～Arsia in Daydream[ドリームキャストコレクション]; on Buyee, ドリコレ or 廉価版 (“budget edition”) means the second. On eBay, “Sample” or “Promo” marks a promotional copy.

Why was there never a Napple Tale 2?

We could not find a reason given anywhere, so we will not offer one. Neither Wikipedia mentions a sequel or a later version, and the Media Arts Database has only the two Dreamcast editions. The budget re-release is the same game, so for this series it does not count as a second part.

Has it been re-released?

Only as that Dreamcast Collection edition. Sega’s own list has no link from the row to a later version, and neither Wikipedia mentions one. English Wikipedia says an unofficial English translation patch was released in 2019; that is a fan patch, not a release.

Because Kataban shows both editions side by side. A daydream world with the seasons laid out side by side, a soundtrack people still single out, and one cheaper reprint: this disc was never followed by a second one. If you want the Japanese original, the code is HDR-0079, and the box without “Dreamcast Collection” on it is the first edition.
