Silhouette Mirage on Sega Saturn: Treasure’s 1997 Action Game, Its Codes T-32901G and T-32903G, and Why It Never Got a Second Part

2026-11-09

  • Sega Saturn
  • Action
  • 1990s
  • ESP

Silhouette Mirage — シルエットミラージュ, printed on the disc as SILHOUETTE MIRAGE — is a side-scrolling action game made by Treasure and released in Japan on 11 September 1997 for the Sega Saturn, published by ESP, product code T-32901G, at 5,800円 before tax. Its heroine, Shyna, carries two opposite attributes, and which one she fights with depends on which way she is facing.

This is the fourth entry in a series about Sega Saturn games that never became a series. Silhouette Mirage was ported once, to the PlayStation, and Treasure later borrowed its central idea for another game — but there was never a Silhouette Mirage 2. On the Saturn it exists in two Japanese editions with two codes: the 1997 first edition and a cheaper 1998 reissue in the Saturn Collection line.

  • Japanese title: シルエットミラージュ (disc: SILHOUETTE MIRAGE), developed by Treasure, published by ESP (エンターテインメント・ソフトウェア・パブリッシング)
  • First edition: 11 September 1997 · 5,800円 before tax · T-32901G
  • Saturn Collection reissue: December 1998 · 2,800円 before tax · T-32903G — the same game at a lower price
  • PlayStation port: シルエットミラージュ 〜リプログラムドホープ〜, Treasure, 23 July 1998; the only Western release was this PlayStation version, in North America in 2000
  • No sequel: Treasure reused the two-attribute idea in Ikaruga (2001), which English Wikipedia calls a spiritual sequel to a different game
  • Buying it: search the code; on Buyee and eBay the title brings up many copies of the free demo disc

What is Silhouette Mirage, and how does it play?

Every enemy is either “Silhouette” or “Mirage”, and only the opposite attribute hurts it. Shyna is both: both Wikipedias say her attribute is set by the direction she faces, so beating an enemy often means getting round to its other side first. They differ on which direction gives which attribute, so we will not say. To move enemies where she needs them, she can grab and throw them; she can also beat money out of them and spend it on the “parasites” that serve as her weapons, and a reflector bounces shots back. Both Wikipedias describe all of this.

Japanese Wikipedia adds that the stages are built from many mid-boss fights, as in Treasure’s Gunstar Heroes and Alien Soldier, and that the game has multiple endings. English Wikipedia quotes the director, Masaki Ukyo, on where it started: the idea of two different attributes, with the grabbing, throwing and money-stealing added later. These are the sources’ descriptions; we have not played through the game to check them.

Which Saturn disc is which: T-32901G or T-32903G?

The first edition is T-32901G. The Media Arts Database has it as row M719657, published on 11 September 1997 by ESP, with the copyright line ©1997 TREASURE/ESP. Sega’s own list of licensed Saturn software gives the same date and code, the publisher as トレジャー／ESP, and the price, 5,800円. Suruga-ya lists it at 6,380円, which is that price with today’s 10% tax: 5,800 × 1.1 = 6,380.

The Saturn Collection reissue is T-32903G. The Media Arts Database has it as row M749416, edition セガサターンコレクション, at 2,800円; Suruga-ya lists it as シルエットミラージュ サタコレシリーズ at 3,080円 (2,800 × 1.1), and the disc database redump lists its serial as T-32903G with the edition “Satakore”. The two give release dates one day apart in December 1998, so we give only the month.

In a listing, サタコレ or サターンコレクション (“Saturn Collection”), or “Satakore” in English, means the 1998 reissue; their absence and a 1997 date usually mean the first edition. The game itself is the same.

There is a third disc to know about, and it is not the game. 体感CD-ROM 操作説明 (a “hands-on CD-ROM” with an instruction guide) is a free demo disc. redump lists it as 610-6679, and Suruga-ya files it as シルエットミラージュ 体感CD-ROM 操作説明[体験版], where 体験版 means “trial version”. On Buyee and eBay it is listed often, as 体験版, “trial” or “demo”. If the price looks low, check for those words.

How is it related to the PlayStation version?

It is the same game, ported. Both Wikipedias give the PlayStation version as シルエットミラージュ 〜リプログラムドホープ〜 (Reprogrammed Hope), released by Treasure itself on 23 July 1998, and the Media Arts Database records the same title and date. Both also say it adds bosses that are not in the Saturn version. English Wikipedia adds that the story was adjusted around them and that some Saturn backgrounds and animations were removed. For this series a port counts as the same game, not a sequel.

The PlayStation version is also the only one that left Japan. English Wikipedia says Sega was not interested in publishing the Saturn game in the West, and that Working Designs translated the PlayStation version and released it in North America in 2000, with a higher difficulty and other changes; redump lists that North American disc as SLUS-00728. Both Wikipedias say the PlayStation version was put on the Japanese PlayStation Network as a download on 25 August 2010.

Why was there never a Silhouette Mirage 2?

We could not find a reason given anywhere, so we will not offer one. What we can set out is what exists. The Media Arts Database has the two Saturn editions and the PlayStation port, and nothing later with the name. English Wikipedia says Treasure announced it as its last Saturn game, then made one more, Radiant Silvergun.

Its central idea did come back. English Wikipedia’s article on Silhouette Mirage says Treasure reused the two-attribute damage mechanic in Ikaruga (2001), where the player’s ship switches between black and white. The article on Ikaruga describes that game differently: as the spiritual sequel to Radiant Silvergun, begun as its direct sequel under the name “Project RS2”, which “borrows” the polarity mechanism from Silhouette Mirage. A borrowed mechanic in a game that continues something else is not a second Silhouette Mirage, and for this series it does not count as one.

Has the Saturn game been re-released?

Only as the 1998 Saturn Collection disc, as far as we could find. Sega’s list of licensed Saturn software has no link or remark on the Silhouette Mirage row, and English Wikipedia mentions no later version of the Saturn game. The PlayStation version is the one that was put back on sale, as the 2010 download.

Why search Kataban for this one?

Because the two Saturn editions and the PlayStation port share one name, and Kataban keeps them apart. T-32901G returns the first edition and T-32903G the Saturn Collection disc, each with the Japanese title next to it to copy into a shop. Silhouette Mirage returns both Saturn editions, and シルエットミラージュ adds the PlayStation version. In second-hand game shops in Japan, such as Book-Off, games are often shelved in Japanese syllabary order, and this one is under シ.

A heroine who changes sides by turning round, two Saturn discs, one port, and an idea that ended up in a different game: Silhouette Mirage never got a second part of its own. If you want it as first sold, the code is T-32901G; the cheaper reissue is T-32903G; and anything that says 体験版 is the demo.
