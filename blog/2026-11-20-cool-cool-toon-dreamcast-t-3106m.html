Cool Cool Toon on Dreamcast: SNK’s 2000 Rhythm Game, Its Code T-3106M, and Why It Never Got a Second Part

2026-11-20

  • Dreamcast
  • Action
  • 2000s
  • SNK

Cool Cool Toon — printed COOL COOL TOON and read クルクルトゥーン (kuru kuru tūn) — is a rhythm game that SNK released in Japan on 10 August 2000 for the Dreamcast, product code T-3106M, at 5,800円 before tax. A boy or a girl is pulled into Cool Cool Town, a world inside the television, and learns to dance there by steering the analogue stick to letters on a circle and pressing the buttons in time.

This is the first entry in a series about Dreamcast games that never became a series. There was never a Cool Cool Toon 2. An English version was planned and then cancelled, and Japanese Wikipedia says it was the last new home-console game the original SNK made before it went under. A companion game came out the same day on the Neo Geo Pocket Color — a partner, not a sequel.

  • Japanese title: COOL COOL TOON, read クルクルトゥーン; the box calls it a リズムコミック (“rhythm comic”); published and developed by SNK
  • Dreamcast GD-ROM: 10 August 2000 · 5,800円 before tax · T-3106M — the Media Arts Database and Sega’s list of licensee Dreamcast games agree on the date and the price, and Sega’s list gives the code
  • Japan only: the North American version planned for early 2001 was cancelled; an English fan translation appeared in 2023
  • No sequel: its partner Cool Cool Jam on the Neo Geo Pocket Color came out the same day, and the town later became a stage in another SNK game
  • Buying it: the code at Suruga-ya, and the reading plus ドリームキャスト on Buyee

What is Cool Cool Toon, and how does it play?

Both Wikipedias describe the same mechanism: the letters A, B, X and Y appear around a large circle, and you move a marker to each with the analogue stick and press the matching button on the beat. Japanese Wikipedia adds the rest: each hit is graded Cool, Nice, Okay or Poor, a run of good hits builds “Beats” that power a “Flitz Boom”, and the stage itself reacts — the background dances when you do well, and at the lowest level the music drops out and the colours drain away. You choose between two heroes, Amp and Spica, and each has a different rival.

English Wikipedia says the composer Yasumasa Yamada wrote four of the songs and found the game fresh to work on because it was built around music, and that the art was led by Ippei Gyoubu; Japanese Wikipedia adds that the disc, opened on a computer, holds Gyoubu’s design sketches and columns by the staff. These are the sources’ descriptions; we have not played through the game to check them.

Is Cool Cool Jam a sequel?

No. Cool Cool Jam (COOL COOL JAM) is a Neo Geo Pocket Color game released alongside Cool Cool Toon on the same day, by English Wikipedia a companion game. Japanese Wikipedia says the two exchange data through the cable that connects a Neo Geo Pocket to a Dreamcast, and the Media Arts Database row lists that cable and the Neo Geo Pocket as recommended peripherals. Two games designed to work together are partners, not a first and second part. Japanese Wikipedia also notes that a later SNK fighting game, NeoGeo Battle Coliseum, has a stage based on Cool Cool Town — a borrowed setting, not a continuation.

Why was there never a Cool Cool Toon 2?

We could not find a reason given anywhere for no sequel, so we will not offer one. What the sources do say is about the company. English Wikipedia says the North American version, aimed at early 2001, was cancelled because of SNK’s impending bankruptcy and the Dreamcast’s declining popularity. Japanese Wikipedia says Cool Cool Toon was the last new game the original SNK developed for a home console, rather than a port. The Media Arts Database has one Dreamcast row for it.

Has it been re-released?

Not that we could find. Neither Wikipedia mentions a later release, and Sega’s list has no link from the row. English Wikipedia says an English fan translation was released in 2023; that is a patch made by fans, not a release. Suruga-ya also lists a shop demo disc, COOL COOL TOON(クルクルトゥーン)[店頭体験版] — 店頭体験版 means a demo for shop displays, not the game.

Why search Kataban for this one?

Because the title on the disc and the name in the shops are not the same. Kataban lists the game under its printed title, so T-3106M, Cool Cool Toon and COOL COOL TOON each return this disc and nothing else, and so do the reading クルクルトゥーン and the spelling クールクールトゥーン, which Kataban knows as other names for it. At the shops the code is still the surest search: Suruga-ya files the game as リズムコミック クルクルトゥーン, and its title search also brings up the demo. In second-hand game shops in Japan, such as Book-Off, games are often shelved in Japanese syllabary order, and this one is under ク.

A dance game with a partner on a handheld, an English version that never shipped, and a company that ran out of time: this disc was never followed by a second one. If you want the Japanese original, the code is T-3106M.
