Twinkle Tale on Mega Drive: The Only Game from Wonder Amusement Studio, Its Code T-61013, and Why It Never Got a Second Part

2026-11-14

  • Mega Drive (Genesis)
  • Shoot 'em up
  • 1990s
  • Wonder Amusement Studio

Twinkle Tale — ツインクルテール — is a top-down shooting game that Wonder Amusement Studio released in Japan on 24 July 1992 for the Mega Drive, product code T-61013, at 7,800円 before tax. You play Saria, a young apprentice magician, running through nine stages and firing spells in eight directions to rescue the great wizards of her land.

This is the second entry in a series about Mega Drive games that never became a series. Twinkle Tale was the only game its publisher ever released, and English Wikipedia says it was also the last game by the studio that made it. There was never a Twinkle Tale 2, and the game was never released outside Japan.

  • Japanese title: ツインクルテール, published by Wonder Amusement Studio (WAS in Sega’s list and the Media Arts Database)
  • Mega Drive cartridge: 24 July 1992 · 7,800円 before tax · T-61013 — the Media Arts Database and Sega’s list of licensee Mega Drive games agree on the date and the price, and Sega’s list gives the code
  • Japan only: no official release abroad; English-translated cartridges sold online are fan-made
  • No sequel: the only game from its publisher, and by English Wikipedia the last from its developer
  • No re-release we could find: it is not on the Mega Drive Mini or the Genesis Mini
  • Buying it: the code at Suruga-ya, and the title plus メガドライブ on Buyee, with the code as a second Buyee search

What is Twinkle Tale, and how does it play?

English Wikipedia describes it as a run-and-gun game seen from above, compared with Kiki Kaikai and Mercs. Saria moves in eight directions; one button shoots, one switches between magic attacks on the fly, and one sets off a magic bomb. Some of the nine stages are straight courses, others branch and hide items in treasure chests, and each ends with a boss. Her life bar grows after every stage. French Wikipedia calls it an on-foot shoot ’em up in a medieval fantasy world, and Sega’s own list files it under シューティング (shooting).

English Wikipedia says Famitsu gave it 21 out of 40 and that reviews, mostly of imported copies, were generally positive. There is no Japanese Wikipedia article on the game. These are the sources’ descriptions; we have not played through the game to check them.

Who made Twinkle Tale?

The publisher is not in doubt: Sega’s list of licensee games and the Media Arts Database both give WAS, and English Wikipedia spells it out as Wonder Amusement Studio, a game label of the record company Toyo Recording, with Twinkle Tale its only release. The developer is less settled. English Wikipedia says the game was made by ZAP Corporation, which then disbanded, and notes that ZAP is not named in the game’s credits or manual; French Wikipedia says Wonder Amusement Studio developed it and Toyo Recording published it. We give both and do not choose.

ZAP is a name this blog has met before: it is one of the two studios in the credits of the PC Engine shooter Dead Moon, the last game of another one-time label. English Wikipedia lists Dead Moon among ZAP’s earlier work.

Why was there never a Twinkle Tale 2?

We could not find a reason given anywhere, so we will not offer one. What the sources show is that nobody was left to make one: by English Wikipedia, the publisher released nothing else and the developer disbanded after this game. The Media Arts Database has one Twinkle Tale row, and neither Wikipedia mentions a sequel.

Has it been re-released?

Not officially, as far as we could find. It is not in English Wikipedia’s list of games on the Genesis Mini and Genesis Mini 2, and neither Wikipedia mentions a later release. What does exist is a fan translation into English, and on eBay it turns up as reproduction cartridges labelled “English Translated” or “Genesis”. Since the game was never released outside Japan, any copy that is not the Japanese T-61013 is one of those.

Why search Kataban for this one?

Because an English search for this game mostly finds reproductions, and Kataban only lists the Japanese release. T-61013, Twinkle Tale, ツインクルテール and the spaced ツインクル テール each return this cartridge and nothing else, with the Japanese title next to it to copy into a shop. In second-hand game shops in Japan, such as Book-Off, games are often shelved in Japanese syllabary order, and this one is under ツ.

One game from its publisher, the last from its developer, and nothing after it: this cartridge was never followed by a second one. If you want the Japanese original, the code is T-61013, and it is the only original there is.
