Buying Retro Games at Hard Off Stores in Japan: Checking a Store’s Stock on Netmall, Tax-Free Since November 2026 and Kataban

2026-11-26

  • Tax-free shopping

Hard Off is a Japanese chain of reuse shops, and some of its stores sell used game consoles and software alongside instruments, audio gear and computers. For visitors, three things are worth knowing: how to see part of a store’s stock online before you go, what we could and could not confirm about tax-free shopping, which has worked by refund since 1 November 2026, and how to buy from Hard Off once you are home. This guide covers those, and how to use Kataban on the shop floor.

  • Check stock first: each store page on Hard Off’s site links to that store’s listings on its online shop, Netmall. Not everything on the shelf is listed.
  • Search by Japanese title: product codes such as GS-9058 returned nothing on Netmall, so copy the Japanese title from Kataban.
  • Tax-free: we found no Hard Off page about it, so ask at the register. Where a shop offers it, you pay the tax first and get it back after a customs check when you leave Japan.
  • From home: Netmall does not accept credit cards issued outside Japan, but Buyee lists Netmall among the sites you can buy from.

Which Hard Off stores sell retro games?

Hard Off’s company site says it runs reuse stores in all 47 prefectures of Japan. Each store has a page on Hard Off’s Japanese site with its address, hours and the goods it handles, so look for ゲーム (games) or レトロゲーム (retro games) there. The pages are in Japanese only; your browser’s translate feature is enough to read them.

In Tokyo, Hard Off has two stores in Akihabara. Store 1’s page lists games on its 2nd floor, and Store 2’s page says the first glass case inside the entrance holds retro games. Our Akihabara chain-store crawl covers both, alongside BOOKOFF and Suruga-ya.

Can you check a Hard Off store’s stock before you go?

Partly. A store page on Hard Off’s site has a link, オフモールで商品を見る (see items on Off Mall), that opens Netmall with only that store’s listings. Store 1’s page also says that some items in the store are not listed online, and the numbers bear that out: on 9 November 2026, Netmall showed 24 game items for Akihabara Store 1 and 8 for Store 2, the store whose page advertises retro games by the door. Treat Netmall as a sample of the shelf, not the whole of it.

To search one store’s listings:

  1. Search the game on Kataban and copy its Japanese title.
  2. Open the store’s page on Hard Off’s site and tap オフモールで商品を見る.
  3. Paste the title into the box labelled 絞り込みキーワード (refine by keyword).

When we tried Netmall on 9 November 2026, product codes did not work: GS-9058 and HVC-RM returned nothing across all stores. Japanese titles did: 魔法騎士レイアース (Magic Knight Rayearth) returned 73 items, listed as セガサターン 魔法騎士レイアース, with the system name in front.

Can you shop tax-free at Hard Off?

We could not confirm it. We found no Hard Off page about tax-free shopping, and the pages we read did not mention it: Hard Off’s company site in Japanese and English, its store list, and the pages of both Akihabara stores, checked on 9 November 2026. Whether a store is tax-free, and whether it charges a fee, is a question for the register.

What does not depend on Hard Off is the national rule. According to the Japan Tourism Agency, the minimum at any tax-free shop is ¥5,000 or more before tax, per day, at the same store, and you buy at the tax-inclusive price.

How does tax-free shopping work since 1 November 2026?

Japan switched to the Refund Method for purchases made on or after 1 November 2026. The Tourism Agency explains it this way: you pay the price with tax included, and the amount equal to the consumption tax is refunded after customs confirms at the airport or seaport that the goods are leaving Japan.

  • The refund is paid by the shop or by a refund service provider it uses, not by the government. The method differs by shop, and the shop explains how to register for the refund when you buy.
  • Within 90 days of the purchase date, present your passport at a tax-free procedure terminal at the airport or seaport when you leave.
  • Before you check in your bags. Once your suitcase has gone to the airline, you cannot do the procedure.
  • Keep every item on the receipt. If one item from a receipt is missing, nothing on that receipt is refunded.

Our guide to the Refund Method covers the airport steps in more detail.

How do you check a game with Kataban in the store?

  1. Open the scanner. gamekataban.com/?scan=1 opens the barcode scanner straight away. Point it at the JAN barcode printed on the back of a disc case or a cartridge box, not at the store’s price sticker, or type the 13 digits under the barcode.
  2. Check the result. Kataban shows the Japanese title and the product code (型番, kataban) printed on the cartridge or disc. Compare both with what you are holding. A bare cartridge has no barcode, so type the code from its label instead.
  3. Compare with Netmall. Under “Buy this game”, “Search Hard-Off” opens Netmall’s game category with the title, across all stores.

If the staff do not speak English, tap “Show to shop staff”. Kataban shows the Japanese title and product code with a question in Japanese, and the staff member taps an answer that appears in Japanese and English. Ask “Tax-free?” first and, if the answer is yes, “Refund how?” before you leave the register.

Store 1’s page also lists junk items of all kinds (各種ジャンク品), and price stickers use condition words in Japanese. Our glossaries explain the common ones: what “junk” means, what “CIB” (box and manual) means and what “Rank B”, “unopened” and “near-mint” mean.

How is Hard Off different from BOOKOFF, Suruga-ya and Mandarake?

The difference for visitors is how much each chain has published. BOOKOFF has an English tax-free page and a store list that marks tax-free stores, and Mandarake has published how it handles the Refund Method. For Suruga-ya and Hard Off, we found neither. See our BOOKOFF guide, our Suruga-ya guide and our guide to Mandarake’s stores and tax-free shopping.

What if you cannot find it in the store?

You can still buy from Hard Off after you get home, but not directly with a foreign card. Netmall’s FAQ says credit cards issued outside Japan cannot be used. Buyee lists netmall.hardoff.co.jp among the sites you can buy from with its “Add to Buyee” tool, and its FAQ says it checks a request and replies within 1 to 3 business days. Our guide to buying without reading Japanese explains how proxy services work.
