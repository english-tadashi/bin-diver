Flying Power Disc on Neo Geo: Data East’s 1994 Disc Game, Its Two Japanese Editions, and the Sequel That Came 28 Years Later

2026-11-25

  • Neo Geo
  • Sports & racing
  • 1990s
  • Data East

Flying Power Disc — フライングパワーディスク, known abroad as Windjammers — is a flying-disc game that Data East released for the Neo Geo in 1994: first in arcades, then on 8 April 1994 as a home cartridge at 25,000円 before tax, and on 20 January 1995 on Neo Geo CD, DECD-002, at 5,800円. Two players stand on either side of a court and throw a disc at each other’s goal zone, faster with every exchange.

It belongs with the games in our series on games that never got a second part, except for one thing: this one did, 28 years later. Windjammers 2 came out on 20 January 2022, made by Dotemu, a company that had not existed when the first game was released. Here is the original, its two Japanese editions, and how the sequel came about.

  • Japanese title: フライングパワーディスク; published by Data East (データイースト)
  • Neo Geo cartridge (AES): 8 April 1994 · 25,000円 before tax · code given two ways in our sources, NGH-065 on the box or DEH-002 on the board — see below
  • Neo Geo CD: 20 January 1995 · 5,800円 before tax · DECD-002, in three sources
  • The sequel: Windjammers 2 (Dotemu, 2022), licensed from Paon DP, which acquired the rights after Data East’s bankruptcy
  • Buying it: the Japanese title at Suruga-ya, and the title plus ネオジオ on Buyee

What is Flying Power Disc, and how does it play?

English Wikipedia compares it to Pong or air hockey. You pick one of six players, each with their own speed, power and special throw, and defend your end of the court while trying to land the disc in the opponent’s goal zone — three points for the yellow zones, five for the red, two to the other side if you let the disc drop. You can bounce it off the walls, lob it, or dive to catch it. A match is the best of three sets. English Wikipedia says that by Famitsu’s figures the Neo Geo CD version sold over 4,307 copies in its first week, and that the game later found a following again, partly through Giant Bomb, which played it on camera and named it its “Old Game of the Year”. These are the sources’ descriptions; we have not played through the game to check them.

Which Japanese release is which: cartridge or CD?

The Media Arts Database has both. The cartridge is row M755469: ネオジオ専用ロムカセット, 8 April 1994, 25000円 (税抜), publisher データイースト株式会社. The CD is row M755470: フライングパワーディスク CD-ROM版, 20 January 1995, 5800円 (税抜). Suruga-ya keeps them apart by a word in brackets, (ROMカセット) or (CD-ROM), and also lists the arcade board, [基板のみ] (“board only”).

The codes need care. For the CD, DECD-002 appears in three places: the disc database redump, the collectors’ database sumthings (with the barcode 4964808500604) and arcade-history. For the cartridge, the sources disagree. sumthings gives the box code as NGH-065, with the barcode 4964808100712, and arcade-history also lists it as NGH-065; the emulator MAME, in its notes on the game, lists the Japanese cartridge board as DEH-002 (and DEM-002 for the arcade one). They may be describing different things — the box and the board — but we have not seen a box ourselves, so we give both as reference only. Neither code is on Suruga-ya’s pages for the game, and neither returns anything on Buyee. Search the title instead.

How did a sequel arrive 28 years later?

Through a company that bought the rights and another that asked to use them. English Wikipedia says that after Data East went bankrupt, the rights to the game were acquired by Paon DP, a company that hired some former Data East staff. Dotemu first ported the original to PlayStation 4 and PlayStation Vita in August 2017 and to Nintendo Switch in October 2018. According to English Wikipedia’s article on the sequel, Dotemu approached Paon DP about both the port and a new game; pre-production on Windjammers 2 began in late 2017, it was announced in a Nintendo Direct in August 2018, and after delays it was released on 20 January 2022 for Nintendo Switch, PlayStation 4, Xbox One, Windows and Stadia. It keeps the six original players and courts and adds new ones.

So this is a real sequel — a new game continuing the first, with the rights holder’s agreement — but not from the company that made the original, which no longer existed. The 2017 and 2018 versions are ports of the 1994 game; the Wii Virtual Console release in Japan (22 June 2010) was too, and English Wikipedia says it was removed on 24 December 2013, the only Neo Geo game delisted from that service.

On eBay, “Windjammers” on a cartridge label usually means the North American release, “MVS” the arcade cartridge, and “conversion” an arcade board rebuilt into a home cartridge — none of them is the Japanese AES original.

Why search Kataban for this one?

Because the code for the cartridge is uncertain and the title is long. On Kataban, フライングパワーディスク and Windjammers each return five rows — the cartridge, the CD, the 2010 Wii download and the 2017 PlayStation 4 and PlayStation Vita downloads — with their systems next to them. The Japanese name Flying Power Disc in Latin letters returns nothing, because Kataban lists the English title as Windjammers. In second-hand game shops in Japan, such as Book-Off, games are often shelved in Japanese syllabary order, and this one is under フ.

A disc game from 1994, a company that went under, and a sequel made by someone else 28 years on. If you want the Japanese original, the CD is DECD-002, and the cartridge is the one with フライングパワーディスク on a Neo Geo box.
