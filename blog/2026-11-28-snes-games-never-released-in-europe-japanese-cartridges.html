Released in America, Never in Europe: Five SNES Games and Their Cheaper Japanese Cartridges

2026-11-28

  • Super Famicom (SNES)
  • Platformer
  • Shoot 'em up
  • Beat 'em up
  • 1990s
  • Nintendo
  • Capcom
  • Tecmo

If you grew up with a PAL Super Nintendo, some of the games American players talk about never reached your shops. The usual answer is to buy the American cartridge. This post is about the other answer: the Japanese Super Famicom cartridge of the same game. For four of the five games below it costs a fraction of the American one, and it fits a European console better.

  • Kirby’s Dream Land 3: 星のカービィ3 · Nintendo · SHVC-AFJJ-JPN · platformer
  • Aero Fighters: ソニックウイングス (Sonic Wings) · Video System · SHVC-HW · vertical shooter
  • Final Fight Guy: ファイナルファイト・ガイ · Capcom · SHVC-FY · beat ’em up
  • Mega Man Soccer: ロックマンズサッカー (Rockman’s Soccer) · Capcom · SHVC-RQ · football
  • Ninja Gaiden Trilogy: 忍者龍剣伝 巴 (Ninja Ryūkenden Tomoe) · Tecmo · SHVC-ANRJ-JPN · action — the exception on price
  • Before you buy: a Japanese cartridge fits a PAL SNES, but needs an adapter or a modified console to start

Why a Japanese cartridge suits a PAL SNES

Because the two are the same shape. When Nintendo brought the SNES to Europe in 1992 it kept the Super Famicom’s design, and ConsoleMods notes that PAL cartridges use the same shells as Japanese ones. American cartridges are a different shape and do not go in at all without an adapter or cutting the console.

What stops a Japanese game from starting on a PAL machine is the lockout chip, not the plastic. The fix is a pass-through adapter, which borrows the chip of a PAL cartridge plugged in behind, or a modification such as a SuperCIC board or a region switch. A guide to the lockout chip widely shared among modders also recommends a 50/60 Hz switch on a PAL console, so Japanese games run full-screen and at their proper speed.

Kirby’s Dream Land 3

The last game Nintendo published for the SNES in North America, released there in November 1997 and in Japan in March 1998. English Wikipedia says problems converting it to PAL kept it out of Europe and Australia for years; Europe first got it on the Wii’s Virtual Console in July 2009, and it has never been sold there on cartridge. Kirby and his animal friends play the same in both versions; the Japanese cartridge is about a seventh of the price of the American one.

Code: SHVC-AFJJ-JPN · See it on Kataban →

Aero Fighters (Sonic Wings)

Video System’s arcade shooter, called Sonic Wings in Japan, came to the Super Famicom in July 1993 and to North America as Aero Fighters in November 1994. SNES Central lists only the American cartridge outside Japan, and The Cutting Room Floor calls it one of the rarest standard North American releases — hence the price gap, the widest in this list. One difference to know: the Japanese version sends you round for a second loop after you beat it, while the American one returns to the title screen.

Suruga-ya spells the title with a small ィ (ソニックウィングス), so the code is the safer search. Code: SHVC-HW · See it on Kataban →

Final Fight Guy

Capcom’s first SNES Final Fight left out Guy. Final Fight Guy, released in Japan in March 1992, puts him back in place of Cody. In North America it was a rental exclusive at Blockbuster stores, later given a limited release, which is why the American cartridge costs over $400. The Japanese one sold in ordinary shops. It is also not censored the way the American version is: as one American reviewer of the Super Famicom version points out, the US version swaps out the female enemies and the alcohol pick-ups.

Code: SHVC-FY · See it on Kataban →

Mega Man Soccer

The only football game in the Mega Man series: Dr. Wily’s robots take over stadiums, and each character has a special shot drawn from the action games, such as Fire Man setting the ball alight. It came out in Japan as Rockman’s Soccer in early 1994 and in North America that April. GiantBomb lists just the two releases. Reviews were mixed at the time, but it is the cheapest way into this list.

Code: SHVC-RQ · See it on Kataban →

Ninja Gaiden Trilogy: the exception

Tecmo put the three NES Ninja Gaiden games on one cartridge in 1995, with updated graphics and music; it came out in North America that August and in Japan as Ninja Ryūkenden Tomoe a day later. GiantBomb lists no European release. This is the one game here where the Japanese cartridge costs more: PriceCharting’s guide was $188.33 against $159.97 for the American one on 14 November 2026, and Suruga-ya’s cheapest copy that day was ¥42,680. The story scenes between stages are also in Japanese on the Japanese cartridge. If you want this one, the American cartridge plus an adapter is the better buy.

Code: SHVC-ANRJ-JPN · See it on Kataban →

Which American-only games are better bought in English?

The text-heavy ones. Chrono Trigger, Final Fantasy VI, EarthBound and Super Mario RPG also never had a PAL cartridge, and their Japanese versions are cheap too — but they are role-playing games you play by reading. Unless you read Japanese, buy those in English.

What about the cost of importing?

Since July 2021 the EU charges import VAT on every parcel from outside it, however small, and since 1 July 2026 a flat €3 customs duty on low-value parcels as well. Budget for both on top of the cartridge and shipping.
