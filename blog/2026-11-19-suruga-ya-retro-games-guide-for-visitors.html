Buying Retro Games at Suruga-ya Stores in Japan: Tax-Free Since November 2026, Store Stock Search and Kataban

2026-11-19

  • Tax-free shopping

Suruga-ya is a Japanese chain of second-hand shops that also runs the online shop Suruga-ya.jp, and several of its stores sell retro games. For visitors, three things are worth knowing before you go: which stores carry games, how to check a store’s stock from your phone, and what we could and could not confirm about tax-free shopping, which has worked by refund since 1 November 2026. This guide covers those, and how to use Kataban on the shop floor.

  • Find a store: Suruga-ya’s store list is split by prefecture, and each store page lists the goods it handles.
  • Check stock first: store pages link to a search of Suruga-ya.jp limited to that store, and you can search it by the product code Kataban gives you.
  • Tax-free: we found no Suruga-ya page about it, so ask at the register. Where a shop offers it, you pay the tax first and get it back after a customs check when you leave Japan.
  • On the shelf: open gamekataban.com/?scan=1, scan the barcode on the box, and check the Japanese title and product code.

Which Suruga-ya stores sell retro games?

Suruga-ya lists its stores by prefecture. Each store page gives the address, opening hours and the goods the store handles (取扱商品), so look for レトロゲーム (retro games) in that line. Some Suruga-ya stores sell only cards, figures or books. The store list and store pages are in Japanese only; your browser’s translate feature is enough to read them.

In Tokyo, the Akihabara Game Hall (駿河屋 秋葉原ゲーム館) is the store to start with. It moved to Soto-Kanda 3-15-4, 2F, and reopened there on 25 September 2026; Suruga-ya’s store page and AKIBA PC Hotline both report the move. Our Akihabara chain-store crawl covers it alongside the other big chains in the area. In Osaka, our Den Den Town guide covers Suruga-ya’s two stores in Nipponbashi.

Can you check a Suruga-ya store’s stock before you go?

Yes, roughly. The store pages of the Akihabara Game Hall and of both Nipponbashi stores in Osaka have a link headed 商品在庫検索 (stock search) that opens Suruga-ya.jp with a filter for that store. Suruga-ya says it shows items the store has in stock, but with two warnings: the prices shown are Suruga-ya.jp prices, and an item may already have sold in the store. For the latest price and stock, it says to ask the store. On another store’s page, look for the same heading.

You can search that store-only list by product code:

  1. Search the game on Kataban and copy its product code with the copy button.
  2. Open the store’s page on Suruga-ya’s site and tap 商品在庫検索.
  3. Paste the code into the box labelled リスト内検索 (search within the list) at the top of the results, and tap 絞り込む (narrow down).

When we tried this on 9 November 2026 with the Akihabara Game Hall, GS-9058 (Magic Knight Rayearth on Saturn) and HDR-0176 (Cosmic Smash on Dreamcast) each returned only that game, with or without the hyphen. T-14418G (Princess Crown) returned nothing for the store, although Suruga-ya.jp as a whole had it. A Japanese title works too, but it brings in related goods: 魔法騎士レイアース returned 7 items for the store. Like the store list, these pages are in Japanese, so use your browser’s translate feature if you need to.

Can you shop tax-free at Suruga-ya?

We could not confirm it. We found no Suruga-ya page about tax-free shopping, and the pages we read did not mention it: Suruga-ya’s store lists for Tokyo and Osaka and the Akihabara Game Hall page, checked on 9 November 2026. Our Osaka guide found the same for the Nipponbashi stores. Whether a store is tax-free, and whether it charges a fee, is a question for the register.

What does not depend on Suruga-ya is the national rule. According to the Japan Tourism Agency, the minimum at any tax-free shop is ¥5,000 or more before tax, per day, at the same store, and you buy at the tax-inclusive price.

How does tax-free shopping work since 1 November 2026?

Japan switched to the Refund Method for purchases made on or after 1 November 2026. The Tourism Agency explains it this way: you pay the price with tax included, and the amount equal to the consumption tax is refunded after customs confirms at the airport or seaport that the goods are leaving Japan.

  • The refund is paid by the shop or by a refund service provider it uses, not by the government. The method differs by shop, and the shop explains how to register for the refund when you buy.
  • Within 90 days of the purchase date, present your passport at a tax-free procedure terminal at the airport or seaport when you leave.
  • Before you check in your bags. Once your suitcase has gone to the airline, you cannot do the procedure.
  • Keep every item on the receipt. If one item from a receipt is missing, nothing on that receipt is refunded.

Our guide to the Refund Method covers the airport steps in more detail.

How do you check a game with Kataban in the store?

  1. Open the scanner. gamekataban.com/?scan=1 opens the barcode scanner straight away. Point it at the JAN barcode printed on the back of a disc case or a cartridge box, not at the store’s price sticker, or type the 13 digits under the barcode.
  2. Check the result. Kataban shows the Japanese title and the product code (型番, kataban) printed on the cartridge or disc. Compare both with what you are holding. A bare cartridge has no barcode, so type the code from its label instead.
  3. Compare with Suruga-ya.jp. Under “Buy this game”, “Search Suruga-ya.jp by code” and “by title” open Suruga-ya’s online shop. That shows Suruga-ya.jp’s price and stock, not the shelf in front of you.

If the staff do not speak English, tap “Show to shop staff”. Kataban shows the Japanese title and product code with a question in Japanese, and the staff member taps an answer that appears in Japanese and English. Ask “Tax-free?” first and, if the answer is yes, “Refund how?” before you leave the register.

Price stickers and shelf labels use condition words in Japanese. Our glossaries explain the common ones: what “junk” means, what “CIB” (box and manual) means and what “Rank B”, “unopened” and “near-mint” mean.

How is Suruga-ya different from BOOKOFF and Mandarake?

The difference for visitors is how much each chain has published. BOOKOFF has an English tax-free page and a store list that marks tax-free stores, and Mandarake has published how it handles the Refund Method. For Suruga-ya, we found neither. See our BOOKOFF guide for visitors and our guide to Mandarake’s stores and tax-free shopping.

What if you cannot find it in the store?

You can still buy from Suruga-ya after you get home. According to eBay Japan and BeeCruise, both in announcements of 20 January 2025, Suruga-ya runs Suruga-ya.JP for Japan and Suruga-ya.com for overseas customers, and since January 2025 it has also sold on eBay, with BeeCruise handling overseas shipping and enquiries. Kataban’s “Buy this game” has a “Search Suruga-ya.com” link.

A search on Suruga-ya.com can come up empty for a game that Suruga-ya.JP still lists. On 9 November 2026, GS-9058 returned nothing on Suruga-ya.com, while the Akihabara Game Hall’s stock search on Suruga-ya.JP found Magic Knight Rayearth. If that happens, look on Suruga-ya.JP through Buyee.

To buy from Suruga-ya.JP itself, use a proxy service. Buyee lists suruga-ya.jp among the sites you can buy from with its “Add to Buyee” tool, and its FAQ says it checks a request and replies within 1 to 3 business days. Our guide to buying without reading Japanese explains how proxy services work.
