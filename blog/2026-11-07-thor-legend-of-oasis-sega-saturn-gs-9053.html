Thor on Sega Saturn: The 1996 Follow-up to The Story of Thor, Its Code GS-9053, and Why the Golden Armlet Stopped at Two Games

2026-11-07

  • Sega Saturn
  • RPG
  • 1990s
  • Sega

Thor — トア 〜精霊王紀伝〜, printed on the box as THOR 精霊王紀伝 — is an action RPG that Sega released in Japan on 26 April 1996 for the Sega Saturn, product code GS-9053, at 5,800円 before tax. You play a young warrior, Leon, who must win over the elemental spirits of the land of Oasis to stop an evil wizard. Its music is by Yuzo Koshiro, and in the West it was sold as The Legend of Oasis.

This is the second entry in a series about Sega Saturn games that never became a series, and it is an unusual one, because Thor was itself the second of two. It followed the 1994 Mega Drive game ストーリー オブ トア 〜光を継ぐ者〜, known abroad as The Story of Thor or Beyond Oasis. English Wikipedia calls the Saturn game that game’s “successor and prequel”; Japanese Wikipedia calls it its sequel. What never came was a third: nothing was built on the Saturn game afterwards.

  • Japanese title: トア 〜精霊王紀伝〜 (box and database: THOR 精霊王紀伝), published by Sega (セガ・エンタープライゼス)
  • Sega Saturn CD: 26 April 1996 · 5,800円 before tax · product code GS-9053 — the Media Arts Database and Sega’s own Saturn software list agree on all three
  • The game before it: the Mega Drive ストーリー オブ トア 〜光を継ぐ者〜, 9 December 1994, G-5543
  • Western releases: The Legend of Oasis in North America and The Story of Thor 2 in Europe, both in 1996
  • Nothing after it: no third game, and no re-release of the Saturn game that we could find — while the Mega Drive game has been re-released many times
  • Buying it: search the code; on Buyee it returns only this game, and Suruga-ya files it under a title missing one character

What is Thor on Saturn, and how does it play?

It is an action RPG played in real time. English Wikipedia describes the core of it: Leon carries a golden armlet, and by firing a “Spirit Ball” from it at an object — water, fire, a shadow — he summons the spirit that object belongs to. The spirits fight, heal or help solve puzzles, and there are six of them to win over, against the four of the Mega Drive game. Weapons come with special moves entered like a fighting game’s, and Leon can use short and long swords and a bow. These details are from English Wikipedia; we have not played through the game to check them.

The music connects the two games directly. The Media Arts Database records the Saturn disc’s copyright lines as MUSIC ©YUZO KOSHIRO 1996 and ©SEGA 1996, and English Wikipedia says the soundtrack was composed by Yuzo Koshiro, as with the original. Japanese Wikipedia names Koshiro as producer and composer of the Mega Drive game, which Ancient developed; English Wikipedia credits Ancient with the Saturn game too. Famitsu’s four reviewers gave it 8, 8, 7 and 8, according to English Wikipedia; we have not seen the review itself.

How is it related to The Story of Thor on Mega Drive?

The sources use different words, so we give them as they are. English Wikipedia’s article on the Saturn game calls it “the successor and prequel” to Beyond Oasis, and its plot section sets the story many years before the events of that game. Its article on the Mega Drive game calls the Saturn one a prequel. Japanese Wikipedia’s article on the Mega Drive game ends with one line: 後に続編としてセガサターン用ソフト『トア 〜精霊王紀伝〜』（1996年）が発売された — “later, as a sequel, the Saturn game Thor (1996) was released”. Successor, prequel or sequel: all three describe the same pair, with the Saturn game second in release order.

English Wikipedia adds two details from the making of it. The game was first announced as Legend of Thor, and it was originally considered for Sega’s 32X add-on soon after the Mega Drive game; Ancient said in 2016 that the idea was dropped before development began. That second point rests on Ancient’s own post, as cited by Wikipedia.

One name to keep apart: Sega’s 1992 Game Gear RPG シャダム・クルセイダー 遥かなる王国 was sold abroad as Defenders of Oasis. It is a different game, with its own row in the Media Arts Database, and we covered it in a separate guide.

Why was there never a third Thor game?

We could not find a reason given anywhere, so we will not offer one. What we can set out is what exists. The Media Arts Database has the Mega Drive game (M726081, G-5543), its Wii download, and the Saturn game (M719563, GS-9053) — and nothing later under either Japanese title. Neither Wikipedia mentions a later game in the series. For this series, that is the test: the Saturn game came after the Mega Drive one, and no game was built on the Saturn one in turn. The pair stopped at two.

Has it ever been re-released?

The Saturn game, not that we could find. The Mega Drive game is the opposite. Japanese and English Wikipedia list the same re-releases: Wii Virtual Console in 2007, Steam in 2012, iOS and Android in 2017, the Sonic’s Ultimate Genesis Collection for PlayStation 3 and Xbox 360 in 2009, and the Mega Drive Mini in 2019. Sega’s own Mega Drive software list links its row to a Virtual Console page.

Sega’s Saturn software list, which links the games it has brought back, has no link on the Thor row: just the date, the genre, 5,800円 and GS-9053. English Wikipedia’s article mentions no version after 1996, and its list of Sega Ages releases does not include it. If you want to play the Saturn game, the 1996 disc is still the way — the Japanese one, or the Western The Legend of Oasis or The Story of Thor 2.

How do you tell the Japanese disc from the Western ones?

By the name and the year. English Wikipedia dates the North American The Legend of Oasis to 31 July 1996 and the European The Story of Thor 2 to 19 September 1996. On eBay those titles bring up mostly Western copies; a listing with Thor, Seirei Oukiden (or Seirei Ohkiden), GS-9053 or “Japan” is the Japanese disc. Suruga-ya lists the North American disc as 北米版 THE LEGEND OF OASIS (国内版本体動作不可) — “North American version, will not run on a Japanese console”.

Suruga-ya’s own title for the Japanese disc is THOR精霊紀伝, one character short of 精霊王紀伝: the 王 (“king”) is missing. Its search still finds the game by the full title, but if you copy the title from Suruga-ya into another shop, add the 王 back.

Why search Kataban for this one?

Because the game has three names abroad and two at home, and Kataban takes any of them. GS-9053, The Legend of Oasis and トア 精霊王紀伝 each return this disc, with the Japanese title next to it to copy into a shop; Beyond Oasis returns the Mega Drive game and its Wii download, and トア on its own puts the Saturn and Mega Drive games at the top. In second-hand game shops in Japan, such as Book-Off, games are often shelved in Japanese syllabary order, and this one is under ト.

A Mega Drive game, a Saturn game that came after it, and then nothing: the story of the golden armlet stopped at two. If you want the second one as it was sold in Japan, the code is GS-9053, and the shop may spell the title without its 王.
