Magic Knight Rayearth on Sega Saturn: Sega’s 1995 Anime RPG, Its Codes GS-9058 and GS-9018, and Why It Never Got a Second Part

2026-11-06

  • Sega Saturn
  • RPG
  • 1990s
  • Sega

Magic Knight Rayearth — 魔法騎士レイアース — is an action RPG that Sega released in Japan on 25 August 1995 for the Sega Saturn, at 4,800円 before tax. It is built on the CLAMP anime of the same name: three schoolgirls on a trip to Tokyo Tower are pulled into the world of Cephiro and asked to become its legendary Magic Knights. It came out in two Japanese editions on the same day, a standard disc, GS-9058, and a first-run limited edition, GS-9018.

This is the first entry in a series about Sega Saturn games that never became a series. The anime had a second season, already on air when the game came out; the Saturn game never had a second part. At least five other Rayearth games appeared on other systems in 1994 and 1995, but English Wikipedia describes them and this one as six completely different games, and none of them continues the Saturn game.

It is also the rare game in this series that did leave Japan. Working Designs translated it for North America, and it came out there in 1998 — by both English and Japanese Wikipedia, as the last Saturn game released in the United States. That English edition is a different product from the Japanese disc, and on eBay the two are listed side by side.

  • Japanese title: 魔法騎士レイアース (also written 魔法騎士 (マジックナイト) レイアース), published by Sega (セガ・エンタープライゼス)
  • Sega Saturn CD: 25 August 1995 · 4,800円 before tax — the Media Arts Database and Sega’s own Saturn software list agree on the date and the price
  • Two codes: standard edition GS-9058, first-run limited edition GS-9018 (with a fan book, sticker and outer box)
  • English version: Working Designs, North America, 1998; the last Saturn game released there
  • No sequel: the other Rayearth games of 1994–1995 are separate games on other systems, and the anime’s second season has no Saturn game
  • No re-release we could find: Sega’s own Saturn list marks the games it has brought back, and this one has no mark
  • Buying it: search the code without the hyphen at Suruga-ya, and the title plus セガサターン on Buyee

What is Magic Knight Rayearth on Saturn, and how does it play?

You move three characters around the map and fight the enemies scattered on it, but only one of them fights at a time. English Wikipedia describes it as tag-team play: the other two follow behind, cannot attack and cannot be hurt, and you can switch the active character at any moment. Hikaru has a sword, Umi a foil and Fuu a bow — a change from the anime, where all three carry swords. Both Wikipedias say this, and both note the game’s other habit: after key events, each girl’s diary gets a new entry you can read.

The story starts and ends where the anime’s first arc does, but the middle is its own. Japanese Wikipedia says the plot differs from both the manga and the anime, with many characters of its own; English Wikipedia says it adds new locations and characters. Both point out the darkest difference: in the game, every one of Zagato’s followers dies. Japanese Wikipedia adds that almost all of it is voiced, that new animation was drawn for the second half, and that a boss you lose to comes back weaker when you try again. Famitsu gave it 26 out of 40 on release, according to English Wikipedia; we have not seen the review itself.

Which Japanese release is which: GS-9058 or GS-9018?

Both came out on 25 August 1995 at the same price, and the Media Arts Database has a row for each. The standard edition is GS-9058 (row M719620, barcode 4974365090586), and it is the code Sega’s own Saturn software list gives for the game. The limited edition is GS-9018 (row M719619, barcode 4974365090180, labelled 初回限定版, “first-run limited edition”). Suruga-ya files them the same way: GS9058 returns only 魔法騎士レイアース [通常版] (standard edition), and GS9018 only 魔法騎士レイアース[限定版] and loose parts of it.

The difference is the extras. The database lists a fan book, a sticker, a flyer and an outer box in the limited edition. Japanese Wikipedia describes the same edition as a box set with a レイアースファンブック (Rayearth fan book) and index stickers for the Saturn’s memory cartridge. In listings, 初回限定, 限定版 or ファンブック付き (“with fan book”) means the limited edition, and 通常版 the standard one. The game on the disc is the same.

Was Magic Knight Rayearth released outside Japan?

Yes, three years later. Working Designs published it in North America in 1998; Wikipedia’s list of Saturn games dates it 11 December 1998 and lists no European release. English Wikipedia says it was one of the first Saturn games announced and became the last one released in North America, mostly because the translation took so long, and the English article on Working Designs says the same. Japanese Wikipedia, in one line: 米国ではサターン最後のソフトとして発売された — “in the US it was released as the last Saturn game”.

It is not a straight translation. English Wikipedia says Working Designs found the Japanese version too easy and made the enemies faster and smarter, dropped the voices for unimportant town dialogue, added voices to the diaries, and replaced the Japanese theme song with an English one sung to the same melody. Those details are from that one article. For a buyer, the practical point is simpler: the American release came with the disc artwork in three designs, one for each heroine, and English listings mention “Hikaru disc”, “Umi disc” or “Fuu disc”, stickers and a registration card. A listing that says 1998, Working Designs, or one of those discs is the English edition, not the Japanese GS-9058.

Why did the Saturn game never get a sequel?

We could not find a reason given anywhere, so we will not offer one. What we can set out is what exists. The Media Arts Database has seven Rayearth game packages: the two Saturn editions; two Game Gear games from Sega, 魔法騎士レイアース (16 December 1994) and 魔法騎士レイアース2 making of magic knight (4 August 1995); and three from Tomy, a Super Famicom game and two for the Game Boy, the second of them 魔法騎士レイアース2nd ミッシングカラーズ. Japanese Wikipedia describes each of them separately — from Sega, a Game Gear RPG with slot-machine battles and a raising simulation; from Tomy, a Super Famicom RPG, a Game Boy RPG and that Game Boy game’s sequel — and English Wikipedia calls all six completely different games, not ports.

The “2” is the one to watch in a listing. The Game Gear 魔法騎士レイアース2 came out three weeks before the Saturn game and follows the first Game Gear game, not this one. The anime’s second season, which was on air in Japan through most of 1995, has no Saturn game at all. For this series that is enough: nothing was built on the Saturn game as its continuation.

One more connection is often mentioned, and we give it with its source. Japanese Wikipedia says the game was made by Sega’s CS2 R&D division, which went on to Sakura Wars, and that the way it moves between floors — changing the scene without a cut or a fade — was the prototype of the screen effects in Sakura Wars and other later games. That is Japanese Wikipedia’s description, not ours, and English Wikipedia’s article on Sakura Wars does not mention Rayearth. A shared technique is not a sequel, and Sakura Wars is its own story.

Has it ever been re-released?

Not that we could find. Sega’s own Saturn software list links the games it has brought back — to a PlayStation 3 remake, an Xbox 360 or PlayStation Vita version, or a title page — and 15 of its rows carry a link. The Magic Knight Rayearth row has none: just the date, the genre, 4,800円 and GS-9058. English Wikipedia’s list of Sega Ages releases does not include it, and its article mentions no version after the 1998 translation. If you want to play it, the 1995 disc or the 1998 English one is still the way.

Why search Kataban for this one?

Because the name covers seven entries on four systems, and the shops do not sort them for you. Search 魔法騎士レイアース on Kataban and each one appears with its system and, where it has one, its code — the two Saturn editions, GS-9058 and GS-9018, next to the Game Gear G-3423, the Super Famicom and the Game Boy games. Magic Knight Rayearth in English finds the Saturn editions too. In second-hand game shops in Japan, such as Book-Off, games are often shelved in Japanese syllabary order, and this one is under マ.

An anime that ran to a second season, five other games that went their own way, and an English version that closed the Saturn’s life in America: this disc was never followed by a second one. If you want the Japanese original, the code is GS-9058, or GS-9018 for the box with the fan book.
