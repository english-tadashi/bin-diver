What Is Mandarake? Tokyo Stores, Language, Tax-Free and Buying from Abroad

2026-11-01

  • Tax-free shopping

Mandarake is a Japanese chain of second-hand shops for manga, anime goods, toys, figures, cosplay and retro video games. It began in 1980 as a tiny used-manga shop in Nakano Broadway, and that is still its home. You can visit its stores in Tokyo or order from its online shop abroad, which has English and Chinese versions.

  • In Tokyo: the main shops are in Nakano, Akihabara, Shibuya and Ikebukuro. Check hours before you go.
  • Language: a travel guide mentions staff who speak English and Chinese, but Mandarake does not say so. Be ready to point and use a translator.
  • From abroad: order directly, pay in yen within 5 days, and expect to pay any import charges yourself.

What is Mandarake?

Manga artist Masuzo Furukawa started it in 1980 in a seven-square-metre shop in Nakano Broadway, and the company was incorporated in February 1987. It later listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange (code 2652). In September 2018, toys were its largest category at 48% of sales.

For game collectors, the main point is that Mandarake is not a game shop first. Games sit beside everything else, so stock and prices depend on the store.

Do Mandarake staff speak Chinese or English?

Some may, but do not count on it. Japan Travel says there are international staff who speak English, Chinese and other languages. That is the only source we found, and Mandarake’s own store pages say nothing about staff languages.

Here is what we can confirm. Mandarake’s online shop and tax-free page link to a Chinese version, and its tax-free page offers official guides in English, Chinese and Korean. A Mandarake press officer told Real Sound in 2024 that many Asian collectors visit its specialist shops. That tells you about the customers, not the staff.

Plan as if nobody speaks your language. Save the Japanese title of what you want, or show the screen in Kataban: search a game and tap “Show to shop staff”.

Can you shop tax-free at Mandarake?

Yes, in store, but the method changed on 1 November 2026. Mandarake’s tax-free page says all stores take part, and the total must be 5,000円 before tax (5,500円 with tax) or more. You need your original passport. A photocopy is not accepted.

You now pay the tax-inclusive price at the register. Mandarake says the store then registers your purchase and gives you instructions, and that you must complete JP Refund registration before leaving Japan. After customs confirms the goods are leaving, the refund is processed. Our guide to the Refund Method explains the airport steps.

Online orders are different. Mandarake says orders shipped overseas are exempt from Japanese sales tax, while domestic orders and in-store pickup have no tax-free option.

How do you buy from Mandarake from abroad?

Use the official online shop and switch to English or Chinese. Its FAQ and shipping pages give these rules:

  • Stock: items belong to a specific store, and you can combine orders only from the same store. Unless the item page says otherwise, you cannot reserve items.
  • Payment: in yen, by 3D Secure credit card, PayPal, Alipay or Amazon Pay, within 5 days of the first invoice. Split payments are not accepted.
  • Cancelling: Mandarake usually does not accept cancellations, and paid orders cannot be changed.
  • Shipping: options include DHL Express, EMS and Air Small Packet, and the choice depends on your country. UPS is for Nakano orders to select US areas. Items usually ship 1 to 2 days after payment. The list you see at checkout is the one that counts.
  • Import charges: you pay any duties and clearance fees on delivery. Mandarake will not undervalue packages and cannot estimate your costs.

If an item cannot ship to you, a proxy or forwarding service is the other route. We explain how those services work in our guide to buying without reading Japanese.
