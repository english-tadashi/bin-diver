Akihabara Retro Game Chain Store Walk

2026-08-23

Why the Chains

The famous Akihabara retro shops get the photos, but the chains get the volume. Janpara, Suruga-ya, Lashinbang, and BOOKOFF all run on the same logic: fixed prices on every tag, condition stated on the label, cards accepted, and no haggling expected. For an overseas buyer that is worth a lot — you can walk in, read a price, and buy, without a single conversation.

They also restock differently from the specialist stores. Chain inventory comes from walk-in trade-ins across the whole city, so what is on the shelf today is close to random. That is exactly why this walk is worth doing in one go: six stops, six independent piles of stock.

The Route

Start at Akihabara Station's Electric Town Exit and head north up Chuo-dori. Janpara's No.5 branch is the far end of the walk, about 6 minutes up, so this route runs out first and then works back down toward the station. From Janpara it is barely a minute across to the Suruga-ya game branch, two more minutes down to the Suruga-ya main building, and another two to Lashinbang in AKIBA Culture Zone. From there it is about 3 minutes south to the Lashinbang inside Radio Kaikan, and 2 more to BOOKOFF right in front of the station — which puts you back where you started. Around 16 minutes of actual walking; budget three hours with browsing.

Store-by-Store Notes

  • Janpara Akihabara No.5 — Janpara is a used electronics chain, so this is the hardware stop: consoles, controllers, cables, and handhelds, all tested and graded before they hit the shelf. The condition grade is printed on the tag, which is the closest thing to a warranty you will get on 30-year-old hardware.
  • Suruga-ya Akihabara (game branch) — Games only, which makes it the densest software stop on the route. Stock runs from Famicom cartridges through to current-gen discs, sorted by system.
  • Suruga-ya Akihabara Main Store — The bigger, broader flagship a couple of minutes down the street. Games share the building with hobby goods, so the game section is smaller than the specialist branch, but the two get different trade-ins and it costs you two minutes to check both.
  • Lashinbang AKIBA Culture Zone — Lashinbang is primarily a doujin and anime-goods chain, and the game shelves are a side business. That is the point: fewer collectors dig here, and the pricing is less picked-over than at the game-first shops.
  • Lashinbang Radio Kaikan — The second Lashinbang, inside the Radio Kaikan tower at the station. Small, and worth it mostly because it is on the way back and stocked separately from the Culture Zone branch.
  • BOOKOFF Akihabara Station Front — The last stop, and the wild card. BOOKOFF prices by algorithm and condition, not by collector demand, so genuinely rare things occasionally sit on the shelf at an ordinary price. Nothing is guaranteed, but this is the store where the surprise happens.

No Japanese? No Problem

Chain shelves are the easiest in Akihabara to read without Japanese, because everything is tagged and sorted by system. The gap is the title itself. For Famicom, Super Famicom, and other cartridge-era games, there is no useful barcode — type the product code (型番) printed on the cartridge label into Kataban and you get the English title back. For PlayStation, Saturn, and Dreamcast discs, the barcode on the case works. Either way you can confirm the game before you carry it to the register.

Practical Tips

Chain stores are the friendliest ones for a foreign passport: most take credit cards, and the larger branches handle tax-free purchases if you bring your passport and hit the minimum spend. Prices are fixed, so there is nothing to negotiate and no reason to feel awkward walking out empty-handed. Opening hours run roughly 11am to 8pm, though the station-front BOOKOFF tends to stay open later than the rest.

One habit worth forming: check the same title at more than one stop. These are six separate inventories priced independently, and the spread on a common game between BOOKOFF and a game-first shop can be substantial.

Before You Go

This is the walk for volume and for luck rather than for a specific grail. Look up your want-list on Kataban first and write down the product codes — on a chain-store shelf where a hundred cartridges face spine-out, the code is what lets you scan the row instead of reading it.
