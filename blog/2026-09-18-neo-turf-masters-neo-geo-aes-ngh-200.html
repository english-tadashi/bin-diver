Neo Turf Masters on Neo Geo AES: Nazca’s Other 1996 Debut and the Sequel That Never Came

2026-09-18

At Tokyo Game Show in September 2026, a golf game from 1996 was back on a show floor — as one of the launch titles for a new console, the NEOGEO AES+ (more on that further down). What follows is about the cartridge it first came out on, thirty years earlier.

In 1996 a thirty-person studio in Osaka shipped two games. One was Metal Slug. The other was a golf game. Neo Turf Masters reached arcades on 29 January 1996 and came home on 1 March as a Neo Geo AES cartridge, product code NGH-200, at 29,800円 before tax, under the Japanese title ビッグトーナメントゴルフ — Big Tournament Golf.

By October that year the studio, Nazca, no longer existed as a company: SNK had absorbed it. Metal Slug became a franchise that is still running. The golf game never got a second round — only ports, a handheld version built by another team, and a modern re-release that had to change its name. This guide covers what the cartridge is, who Nazca were, why the code NGH-200 is the one number you should not type into a shop search, and how to find the cartridge by name instead.

  • Japanese title: ビッグトーナメントゴルフ (Biggu Tōnamento Gorufu), developed by Nazca Corporation (ナスカ), published by SNK
  • Neo Geo AES (home cartridge): 1 March 1996 · product code NGH-200 · 29,800円 before tax, as recorded by the Media Arts Database
  • Arcade (Neo Geo MVS): 29 January 1996 · board number NGM-200
  • Also on disc: Neo Geo CD, 3 May 1996, 7,800円 before tax — the same game, a cheaper format
  • Not the same game: the Neo Geo Pocket Color version (29 July 1999, 3,800円 before tax) was built by Saurus, with cartoonish graphics rather than the arcade game’s digitised art
  • One code, two spellings: at Suruga-ya drop the hyphen — NGH200 returns two listings with sold-out items shown and both are this cartridge, while NGH-200 returns other Neo Geo games and never this one

What is Neo Turf Masters, and why does it play so fast?

It is an arcade golf game with almost nothing between you and the swing. You pick one of six golfers and play stroke play alone or against a second player, or match play head to head, across four fictional courses set in the United States, Japan, Australia and Germany.

Where its contemporaries used a two-click swing meter to set hook and slice, this one splits the job: the first click sets power, the second sets the height of the shot, and you choose hook or slice with the B and C buttons before you swing. The English Wikipedia article sums up the trade: it “makes the game much easier to pick up and play than its more technically demanding contemporaries, but it compensates for this reduced difficulty with fiendish (if somewhat unrealistic) course layouts and highly variable wind.”

It worked. In Japan, Game Machine listed it as the twelfth most successful arcade game of March 1996. Twelve years later, reviewing the Wii Virtual Console release, IGN gave it 7.5 out of 10: “If you’re the type of gamer who appreciates SNK’s arcade style and can jibe with the idea of juiced-up, high-speed golf, Neo Turf might be well worth your 900 Wii Points”.

Who were Nazca, and what happened to them?

Nazca Corporation (株式会社ナスカ) was founded in May 1994 in Suita, Osaka, “by a group of arcade manufacturer Irem employees after the shutdown of its video game division”. It had about thirty employees in 1996. Its producer on this game was Takashi Nishiyama, and the English Wikipedia article on the game calls it “one of the first two titles by Nazca before being acquired by SNK; the other series being the popular Metal Slug”.

The Irem lineage shows. Wikipedia notes that the game “is in many ways similar to Major Title 2: Tournament Leader by Irem”, because “the game was programmed by a group of former Irem employees who had already worked on Major Title 2”. The golf game was, in effect, the sequel to something the same people had made at a company that no longer wanted to make games.

Nazca “became a subsidiary of SNK in 1996”, and Wikipedia’s company infobox gives the studio as defunct in October 1996, with the outcome recorded as “Acquired by SNK then folded”. The staff kept working inside SNK on Metal Slug; the team dispersed after SNK’s own bankruptcy in 2001, some of them to Granzella and PlatinumGames.

Why was there never a sequel?

Because the studio had nine months of independent life and one of its two debut games turned out to be Metal Slug. When SNK absorbed Nazca, it absorbed the people, and the people went where the franchise was. Neo Turf Masters has no second game on any platform, in any decade — the English Wikipedia article on it lists only ports and re-releases.

What it got instead was a long afterlife. The Neo Geo version appeared in SNK Arcade Classics Vol. 1 for PlayStation 2, Wii and PSP, and on the Wii Virtual Console — the Media Arts Database dates the Japanese Virtual Console release to 8 July 2008 at 900 Wii Points. DotEmu put a standalone port on iOS and Android in 2016. Hamster brought it back in the ACA NeoGeo line for PlayStation 4 and Xbox One on 23 February 2017 and for Nintendo Switch on 23 March 2017, at 823円 including tax.

That last release carries a small, telling detail. Outside Japan the ACA version is not called Neo Turf Masters at all: Wikipedia records that Hamster re-released it “under the title Big Tournament Golf, due to a trademark conflict with Augusta National Golf Club over the ‘Masters’ name”. Thirty years on, the Japanese title is the one that travels.

Which release is the Japanese cartridge, and what is its code?

Neo Geo releases carry two numbers with the same digits: NGM- for the MVS arcade board and NGH- for the AES home cartridge. Here they are NGM-200 and NGH-200. The Neo Geo Master List dates both the Japanese and the English AES release to 1 March 1996 and the CD to 1 May 1996; the Media Arts Database dates the Japanese disc to 3 May 1996, so the two sources disagree by two days on the CD and agree on the cartridge.

Kataban’s catalog tracks the Japanese cartridge, row M754660 in the Media Arts Database, published by SNK at 29,800円 before tax. That row has no product code of its own, and neither does the Neo Geo CD row. We added NGH-200 to the catalog on 16 September 2026 from the Master List and the Japanese store listing at satakore.

One more thing that looks like a sequel and is not: the Neo Geo Pocket Color version from July 1999. It is the same idea on different hardware, built by Saurus rather than Nazca, and Wikipedia describes it as having “cartoonish, less detailed graphics compared to the Neo Geo version”. It was reissued in Neo Geo Pocket Color Selection Vol. 1 in 2021, which is why that compilation keeps turning up in searches for this game.

Where can you buy the Japanese cartridge today?

Search the code, but drop the hyphen. Written as it is printed, NGH-200 is useless as a search term at Suruga-ya: on 18 September 2026 it returned 15 listings in the default view and 95 with sold-out items shown, and not a single one was Big Tournament Golf — they were The King of Fighters, Sengoku, Metal Slug 5 and other Neo Geo titles. Written without the hyphen it is exact: NGH200 returned two listings with sold-out items shown, and both were this game’s AES cartridge. In the default view it returns nothing at all — not because the code is wrong, but because neither cartridge was in stock that day. That is a pattern we have seen with other Neo Geo cartridges too: the code points at the right object and the shelf is empty. The code button on this game’s catalog card stays switched off, because the catalog sends the hyphenated form.

The Japanese title is the other way in — and it is not the name on your box art. In Japan this game is filed as ビッグトーナメントゴルフ, “Big Tournament Golf”; Neo Turf Masters is the overseas name, and there is no Japanese rendering of it in use — searching ネオターフマスターズ on Buyee on 18 September 2026 returned nothing at all. ビッグトーナメントゴルフ at Suruga-ya returned 6 listings in the default view that day and 29 with sold-out items shown. Note what that means: the six in-stock listings were the Neo Geo Pocket version (a 美品, near-mint, at ¥7,000 used), a Neo Geo CD copy, a Pocket Color demo cart and three compilations. The AES cartridges — ビッグトーナメントゴルフ(ROMカセット), one 箱説なし (no box or manual), one with a damaged package — were all in the sold-out half of the list, alongside CD-ROM copies, arcade boards and manuals on their own.

Buyee, same story. ビッグトーナメントゴルフ returned 18 listings and ビッグトーナメントゴルフ ネオジオ returned 11, and what was actually there that day was an MVS arcade cartridge, Neo Geo CD copies, the Pocket version, ACA compilations for Switch and a bare Neo Geo motherboard — no AES home cartridge at all. Searching the English name is worse: Neo Turf Masters returned exactly one listing, and it was the motherboard. Re-measured on 18 September 2026: 17 and 10 listings, still not one AES cartridge.

So read the format, and be patient. ロムカセット or ROM is the AES cartridge; CD-ROM is the 1996 disc; 基板 is an arcade board; ネオジオポケット is the 1999 handheld game; 説明書のみ is a manual on its own. The AES cartridge is not a rare game, but on the day we looked it was not in stock at either shop. Prices here are a snapshot of 16–18 September 2026, not a going rate.

What about the new NEOGEO AES+ cartridge?

At Tokyo Game Show 2026, PLAION REPLAI’s booth was given over to the NEOGEO AES+, a new home console the company is developing with SNK, and all ten titles due to launch alongside it were playable. ビッグトーナメントゴルフ is one of the ten, GAME Watch reported on 18 September 2026. The console was first announced for 12 November 2026 and has since been delayed to 16 September 2027, with PLAION REPLAI citing global component supply; it costs 32,800円, or 49,800円 for the Anniversary Edition. PLAION REPLAI’s Japanese store lists the golf game as a “NEOGEO AES+ Game Cartridge” at ¥9,980, shipping from 16 September 2027, “compatible with NEOGEO AES+ and NEOGEO AES video game systems” — and in its Japanese and European stores alike, it sells it as Big Tournament Golf. The Japanese title keeps travelling.

That is a new cartridge, not the one this guide is about. It carries the same game, but it is made now and sold new at a fixed price, and nothing on PLAION REPLAI’s product page ties it to NGH-200. Everything measured above — two sold-out listings at Suruga-ya under NGH200, not one AES cartridge on Buyee — is about stock that was made in 1996, and a new production run in 2027 adds nothing to that stock. GAME Watch’s booth report draws the same line for the whole system: original Neo Geo cartridges have risen sharply in price, it notes, with the first Metal Slug trading at several million yen. We have no such figure for Big Tournament Golf — on the days we looked, neither shop had an original cartridge to put a price on — so read the ¥9,980 as the price of the new cartridge only, not as a guide to what the 1996 one is worth, in either direction. Once the new one ships, a search for ビッグトーナメントゴルフ may bring up both; if a listing mentions AES+ or PLAION, it is the 2027 cartridge.

Why search Kataban for this one?

Because five different releases share one Japanese name, and the catalog keeps them apart. Checked in a desktop browser on 16 September 2026, ビッグトーナメントゴルフ returns five results: the AES cartridge, the Neo Geo CD, the Neo Geo Pocket version, the Wii Virtual Console release and the ACA NeoGeo version on PlayStation 4. Neo Turf Masters returns four of them — the ACA row is catalogued under its Japanese name only. NGH-200 returns exactly one: the 1996 cartridge, with its code, its date and the kana to look under on a Japanese shelf (ヒ). The code that fails at the shop still works here, which is the whole point of writing it down.

Nazca existed for about two and a half years and left behind a run-and-gun series that never stopped and a golf game that never continued. The golf game is still the better argument for the Neo Geo as a home machine: four courses, six golfers, two clicks per swing. If you want the 1996 cartridge, look for ビッグトーナメントゴルフ — and keep NGH-200 for the catalog.
