Comix Zone on Mega Drive: Sega Technical Institute’s 1995 Comic-Book Brawler, Its Code G-4132, and Why It Never Got a Second Part

2026-11-19

  • Mega Drive (Genesis)
  • Beat 'em up
  • 1990s
  • Sega

Comix Zone — コミックスゾーン — is a side-scrolling beat ’em up that Sega released in Japan on 1 September 1995 for the Mega Drive, product code G-4132, at 7,800円 before tax. You play Sketch Turner, a comic artist pulled into the pages of his own comic, fighting from panel to panel against the villain he drew. It was made in America, by Sega Technical Institute, and came out in North America first.

This is the seventh and last entry in a series about Mega Drive games that never became a series. There was never a Comix Zone 2: the game was re-released many times, and in 2022 a film was announced, but no second game. At the end of this guide we look back at all seven Mega Drive games in the series.

  • Japanese title: コミックスゾーン, published by Sega, developed by Sega Technical Institute in the United States
  • Mega Drive cartridge: 1 September 1995 · 7,800円 before tax · G-4132 — the Media Arts Database and Sega’s own Mega Drive software list agree on the date and the price, and Sega’s list gives the code
  • Other releases: North America in July 1995 and Europe in October, before and after the Japanese one
  • A small Japanese run: both Wikipedias say few copies were made in Japan, which is why the cartridge became expensive
  • No sequel: many re-releases and a film announced in 2022, but no second game
  • Buying it: the Japanese title at Suruga-ya and on Buyee — where the Genesis and European copies are mixed in

What is Comix Zone, and how does it play?

Each stage is a comic page. Japanese Wikipedia describes it: you start in the top-left panel and work your way to the bottom-right, the route through the panels can branch, and the ending changes depending on whether you save the heroine. Enemies are drawn onto the page by the villain’s hand, speech appears in balloons, and a hidden move lets Sketch tear a piece of the page and fold it into a paper plane to throw. Both Wikipedias count six stages, in three episodes of two. English Wikipedia says critics praised the look and the music and complained of the difficulty and the short length; Japanese Wikipedia gives Famicom Tsūshin’s 30 out of 40, a Silver Hall of Fame award, and 20.1 out of 30 from Mega Drive Fan readers. These are the sources’ descriptions; we have not played through the game to check them.

English Wikipedia tells how it began: the programmer Peter Morawiec made a demonstration video in 1992, Joe Pencil Trapped in the Comix Zone, inspired by the music video for a-ha’s “Take On Me”. Sega of America’s Tom Kalinske approved it, the work was put on hold for Sonic Spinball, and development started in late 1993. Japanese Wikipedia tells the same story and adds that the Japanese release is a localisation of the American game.

Which release is the Japanese one?

The Japanese cartridge is G-4132: Sega’s list gives 9月 1日｜コミックスゾーン｜アクション｜7,800円｜G-4132｜16Ｍ, and the Media Arts Database row M748382 the same date and price. English Wikipedia says it came out in North America in July 1995 and in Europe on 6 October 1995, and that the Japanese release had a small print run and later became an expensive collector’s item; Japanese Wikipedia says very few were shipped, with the Saturn already on sale by then.

That is why the Western copies dominate the shops. At Suruga-ya the title search returns GENESIS版 COMIX ZONE and MEGADRIVE(EU)版 COMIX ZONE next to the Japanese cartridge, some marked ボーナスCD欠品 (bonus CD missing) — English Wikipedia says Sega bundled a rock CD with the game to promote it. Japanese Wikipedia also says it was the second-to-last Mega Drive game Sega released. On Buyee, “GENESIS”, 北米版 or 欧州版 means a Western copy.

Why was there never a Comix Zone 2?

We could not find a reason given for no sequel, so we will not offer one. English Wikipedia does say the game was not a commercial success, partly because it came so late in the Mega Drive’s life, after the Saturn and the PlayStation; one of its developers recalled that it failed to break even. Neither Wikipedia mentions a sequel. In August 2022, English Wikipedia says, Sega announced a film adaptation with Picturestart. A film is not a second game, and we have not found news of its release.

Has it been re-released?

Many times, as the same game. English Wikipedia lists a Windows port in 1995; an unlockable copy in the Japanese Sonic Mega Collection (2002), pushed by Sonic Team’s Yojiro Ogawa, a fan; the Wii Virtual Console (2007), Xbox Live Arcade (2009), Steam (2010), PlayStation Store (2011), Sega Forever (2017) and Nintendo Classics (2022); and Sega Genesis Classics. Japanese Wikipedia adds the Mega Drive Mini (2019), and says its place as Sega’s second-to-last Mega Drive game was one reason it was chosen. None of these is a new game.

Where can you buy the Japanese cartridge today?

On 17 October 2026 we counted 3 listings of the Japanese cartridge among 13 Buyee results and 1 in stock at Suruga-ya; eBay’s search returned 25 results, mostly Genesis and European copies.

Why search Kataban for this one?

Because in the shops the Japanese cartridge is the rare one, and Kataban lists only Japanese releases. G-4132 returns this cartridge alone; Comix Zone and コミックスゾーン return three rows — this cartridge and the Wii and PlayStation 3 downloads — each with its system. In second-hand game shops in Japan, such as Book-Off, games are often shelved in Japanese syllabary order, and this one is under コ.

What did the seven Mega Drive games have in common?

Each of the seven stopped after one game, for its own reasons:

  • Alisia Dragoon (T-45033, 1992) — its system came from Thexder, and nothing was built on it
  • Twinkle Tale (T-61013, 1992) — the only game from its publisher
  • Eliminate Down (T-73033, 1993) — the only game from its developer, reissued thirty years later
  • Pulseman (G-4098, 1994) — its signature move went on to Pikachu
  • Dynamite Headdy (G-4117, 1994) — ported and reissued, never continued
  • Ristar (G-4126, 1995) — a character who returned only in cameos
  • Comix Zone (G-4132, 1995) — a film announced, a second game never

Three from licensees, two of them the only game of the company that made or published them, and four from Sega itself in the console’s last years — and none of the seven was followed by a second part of its own. That is the Mega Drive edition of this series. From 20 November it moves forward to the last Sega console, with Dreamcast games that never became series either.
