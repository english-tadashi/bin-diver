Ristar on Mega Drive: Sega’s 1995 Shooting Star, Its Code G-4126, and Why It Never Got a Second Part

2026-11-18

  • Mega Drive (Genesis)
  • Platformer
  • 1990s
  • Sega

Ristar — リスター・ザ・シューティングスター (Ristar the Shooting Star) — is a side-scrolling action game that Sega released in Japan on 17 February 1995 for the Mega Drive, product code G-4126, at 6,800円 before tax. You play Ristar, a young shooting star whose arms stretch in eight directions to grab enemies, swing from poles and launch himself through the air.

This is the sixth entry in a series about Mega Drive games that never became a series. English Wikipedia says it plainly: Ristar never received any sequels. The character has since made cameo appearances in other Sega games, and the game itself has been re-released many times — but there was never a Ristar 2.

  • Japanese title: リスター・ザ・シューティングスター, published by Sega; abroad simply Ristar
  • Mega Drive cartridge: 17 February 1995 · 6,800円 before tax · G-4126 — the Media Arts Database and Sega’s own Mega Drive software list agree on the date and the price, and Sega’s list gives the code
  • Game Gear cartridge: the same day · 3,800円 · G-3365, by Sega’s Game Gear list — a different game under the same name
  • Who made it: Japanese Wikipedia says Sonic Team; English Wikipedia says Sega marketing staff said in 1994 that it was not the Sonic programming team
  • No sequel: only cameos, such as starting the races in Sonic & All-Stars Racing Transformed (2012)
  • Buying it: the full Japanese title at Suruga-ya and on Buyee

What is Ristar, and how does it play?

English Wikipedia compares it with Super Mario and Sonic, but with less jumping and speed and more use of the arms: Ristar grabs an enemy and pulls himself into it in a headbutt, opens treasure chests the same way, and swings around “Star Handles” until he flies off in a “Meteor Strike” that destroys anything he touches. Japanese Wikipedia counts seven rounds of three areas each, across six planets of a star system far from Earth, against an alien tyrant, Greedy, with twelve hidden treasures that unlock passwords. Mega Drive Fan readers gave it 20.8 out of 30, by English Wikipedia. These are the sources’ descriptions; we have not played through the game to check them.

English Wikipedia also tells where the character came from. When Sega was looking for a mascot to rival Mario, one rejected idea was a rabbit whose long ears could pick things up; the team chose a hedgehog instead. Years later a prototype called Feel, with a character that grabbed things with its arms, became Ristar.

Who made Ristar?

The two Wikipedias tell it differently. Japanese Wikipedia says it was developed by Sonic Team, and lists its designers, its composer and its character designer, Yuji Uekawa, who later designed the modern Sonic. English Wikipedia says that in a 1994 interview with Electronic Gaming Monthly, Sega marketing staff stated that Ristar was not designed by the Sonic programming team, though much of its staff went on to make Nights into Dreams, and that journalists later came to credit it to Sonic Team. We give both and do not choose.

Which version is which: Mega Drive, Game Gear or Western?

The Japanese Mega Drive cartridge is G-4126: Sega’s list gives 2月17日｜リスター・ザ・シューティングスター｜アクション｜6,800円｜G-4126｜16Ｍ, and the Media Arts Database row M758460 the same date and price. The Game Gear cartridge is G-3365, released the same day at 3,800円; English Wikipedia says it has different level design and gameplay mechanics, so it is a separate game, not the Mega Drive one made smaller.

The Western release changed details. English Wikipedia says the Japanese version has a star mother, Oruto, who sends Ristar, while the international one drops her and mentions his father instead; and that the ice-world boss, a cat robot in Japan — beaten with hot food, a joke on cats’ dislike of hot things — became an ice monster abroad. Suruga-ya files the foreign cartridges as GENESIS版 Ristar and 韓国版 Ristar the shooting star (Korean version). A copy titled only “Ristar” is a Western one.

Why was there never a Ristar 2?

We could not find a reason given anywhere, so we will not offer one. English Wikipedia says the release was overshadowed by the end of the Mega Drive’s life and the arrival of the Saturn and other new consoles, but does not link that to a sequel. What carried on is the character: English Wikipedia mentions cameos in other Sega games, and Japanese Wikipedia names one, Sonic & All-Stars Racing Transformed (2012), where Ristar starts the races. A cameo is not a second part.

Has it been re-released?

Many times, as the same game. Japanese Wikipedia lists the Wii Virtual Console (2006), Steam (2010), Android and iOS (2017), and the compilations Sonic Mega Collection (2002), Sonic Mega Collection Plus (2004) and Sonic’s Ultimate Genesis Collection (2009). It is on the Genesis Mini 2 sold in the West, but not on the Japanese Mega Drive Mini 2, according to English Wikipedia’s list. None of these is a new game.

Why search Kataban for this one?

Because the short name is a trap: リスター alone returns hundreds of unrelated products at Suruga-ya, and Ristar there finds only Western and Korean copies. On Kataban, G-4126 returns this cartridge alone, and リスター・ザ・シューティングスター returns three rows — Mega Drive, Game Gear and the 2006 Wii download — each with its system. Ristar finds the same three, plus one unrelated title that happens to contain “tristars”. In second-hand game shops in Japan, such as Book-Off, games are often shelved in Japanese syllabary order, and this one is under リ.

Stretching arms, a mascot that might have been, and a name that kept turning up as a cameo: this cartridge was never followed by a second one. If you want the Japanese Mega Drive original, the code is G-4126, and the title is the long one.
