Biohazard on GameCube and Game Boy Color: The Japanese Cartridges From Spring 2002

2026-09-23

Capcom’s original biohazard came home twice in Japan in the space of one week in March 2002: as a ground-up GameCube remake on the 22nd, and as a side-story on Game Boy Color, BIOHAZARD GAIDEN, on the 29th. A new Resident Evil movie — Sony Pictures’ live-action reboot directed by Zach Cregger — hit US theaters on 18 September 2026. It doesn’t adapt either 2002 game directly: Cregger has said the story runs parallel to the Raccoon City outbreak of Resident Evil 2 and 3, in an alternate timeline, rather than retelling any specific game. Still, it’s a fair prompt to look back at the month Capcom put biohazard everywhere on Japanese shelves at once. This guide covers what each 2002 release is, how they differ, and how to find the cartridges by product code: DOL-R-GBIJ for the GameCube disc, CGB-BIOJ for the Game Boy Color cartridge.

Movie: Wikipedia, “Resident Evil (2026 film)”

  • GameCube remake: biohazard, 22 March 2002 · product code DOL-R-GBIJ · Capcom’s first game on the console · two 8cm discs
  • Game Boy Color spin-off: BIOHAZARD GAIDEN, 29 March 2002 · product code CGB-BIOJ · developed by the UK studio behind the series’ scenario work, not Capcom’s in-house team · a side story starring Barry Burton, set after 2 and 3
  • Movie tie-in: Resident Evil (2026), dir. Zach Cregger · US theaters from 18 September 2026 · an original story running parallel to 2 and 3, not an adaptation of either 2002 release covered here
  • Today, one is common and one is scarce: the GameCube remake turns up used from about ¥430; GAIDEN cartridges routinely sell for ¥7,000–¥50,000​—​measured 22 September 2026, see below

What are the two Japanese biohazard releases from March 2002?

The GameCube game, titled simply biohazard in lowercase, is a full remake of the original 1996 PlayStation game: new models, a redesigned mansion with added rooms and an expanded outdoor area, re-recorded dialogue. The Japanese Wikipedia article on the series notes it was Capcom’s first game on the GameCube, and, like biohazard 0 after it, shipped on two 8cm discs — the Wii port later fit the whole game on one 12cm disc. It released on 22 March 2002 at a list price of ¥7,480.

BIOHAZARD GAIDEN is a different kind of release: a portable side story, not a remake of anything. According to the Japanese-language BIOHAZARD_GAIDEN encyclopedia entry on Pixiv, it was the series’ first game on a handheld, released 29 March 2002, and its story is set after the Raccoon City incident of biohazard 2 and 3 — though, as the same entry notes, later games left it out of series continuity, so it sits outside the numbered timeline as its own parallel story. It stars Barry Burton, the marksman from the original game, teaming up with Leon S. Kennedy to investigate a cruise ship.

How does the GameCube remake differ from the arcade… er, the original PlayStation game?

Beyond the graphical overhaul, the Japanese Wikipedia article on the 2002 GameCube game (a redirect page within the broader series article) notes two concrete differences from the 1996 original: the game gained the CERO content rating system that didn’t exist at the PlayStation game’s release, and in the Japanese GameCube version specifically, humanoid enemies can be decapitated — a level of gore the international versions didn’t carry, since some voice lines and the hardest difficulty setting were added for releases outside Japan rather than present in the Japanese disc.

Who actually made BIOHAZARD GAIDEN, and is it any good?

Capcom wrote the scenario, but development was outsourced to the UK studio then known as Virgin Games (by release, M4 Limited). The Japanese Wikipedia article on GAIDEN records a mixed critical reception: Japan’s Famitsu Cross Review gave it 22 out of 40 points, and the article summarizes the common complaint as the cast and setting feeling unlike the mainline series — “キャラクターも何もかもが本家シリーズと違うため、バイオハザードファンには非常に違和感がある” (“the characters and everything else are so different from the main series that it feels very off to biohazard fans”). It plays differently too: exploration happens in an original top-down, diagonally-scrolling view rather than the fixed camera angles of the mainline games, alternating with separate battle scenes — a structure closer to an RPG than the rest of the series.

Why is a 2002 Game Boy spin-off harder to find than the flagship GameCube remake?

GAIDEN was Capcom’s only biohazard release on a Game Boy platform, on hardware nearing the end of its life just as the Game Boy Advance took over, from a developer outside Capcom’s main team, with a mixed critical reputation. None of that made it a system-seller at the time — but all of it makes it the kind of cartridge that didn’t get reprinted or bundled the way the GameCube flagship was. The GameCube remake, by contrast, was Capcom’s console debut on new hardware and sold in volume for years, including in a memory-card bundle edition that keeps supply high even today. The price gap below is the result.

What are the product codes, and how do you use them?

The GameCube disc is DOL-R-GBIJ. Two independent sources give it: the Japanese game database Retro Game Hall of Fame (レトロゲームの殿堂) lists it under the game’s catalog entry, and the Internet Archive’s complete art-scan upload is titled “Biohazard GameCube DOL-R-GBIJ(JPN) NTSC-J”.

The Game Boy Color cartridge is CGB-BIOJ. Hard-Off’s Off-Mall listings show it on the cartridge’s product page, and the Internet Archive again gives an independent match: “Biohazard Gaiden [CGB-BIOJ](Game Boy Color)(JP)” for its scanned manual and box.

Both codes are more reliable search terms than the title. バイオハザード alone on any Japanese shop returns every numbered game in the series plus 0, Code: Veronica and the demo discs; DOL-R-GBIJ or CGB-BIOJ narrows a search to exactly one cartridge.

Where can you buy the Japanese cartridges today?

Suruga-ya: the GameCube game is common, GAIDEN is not. On 22 September 2026, a DOL-R-GBIJ code search returned five listings, one in stock — a memory-card-bundle copy missing its memory card, at ¥430, with used marketplace copies from other sellers running ¥500–¥2,700. A CGB-BIOJ search returned a single listing, in stock, priced ¥6,900–¥34,300 depending on condition — against a ¥4,620 original list price — with marketplace copies starting around ¥6,560.

Buyee: search the GAIDEN title on its own. バイオハザードGAIDEN returned 24 listings on 22 September 2026, effectively all of them the Game Boy Color cartridge, priced from about ¥7,800 for a cartridge alone up to ¥52,000 for a boxed, complete copy. バイオハザード ゲームキューブ is far noisier — 280 listings that mix in every numbered GameCube biohazard release, so it’s worth adding a distinguishing word (the memory-card bundle’s name, or the code itself) if you want the 2002 remake specifically.

eBay: GAIDEN commands collector prices, the GameCube game doesn’t. Searching biohazard gaiden game boy (measured 22 September 2026, from Japan) returned 39 results, nearly all genuine GAIDEN cartridges, priced from roughly ¥14,700 for a cartridge alone to nearly ¥190,000 for a complete-in-box copy. biohazard gamecube japan returned 245 results across every numbered game in the series — a title search alone won’t isolate the 2002 remake there, so cross-check listings against the code or the memory-card-bundle title.

Why search Kataban in Japanese?

Because バイオハザード alone returns the whole series, and the English name Resident Evil is a translation the Japanese catalog listings don’t use at all — both games were sold in Japan only under the biohazard name.
