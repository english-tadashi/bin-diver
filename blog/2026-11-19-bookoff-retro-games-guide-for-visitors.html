Buying Retro Games at BOOKOFF in Japan: Tax-Free Refunds Since November 2026 and Scanning Boxes With Kataban

2026-11-19

  • Tax-free shopping

BOOKOFF is Japan’s largest chain of second-hand shops, and many of its stores sell used games, from cartridges to disc-era titles. For visitors, two things are different from a game shop at home: the shelves are labelled in Japanese, and since 1 November 2026 tax-free shopping works by refund, so you pay the tax first and get it back when you leave Japan. This guide covers what we could confirm about tax-free shopping at BOOKOFF, and how to use Kataban in the aisle to match a Japanese box to the game you want.

  • Find a tax-free store: BOOKOFF’s English tax-free site lists stores by region and marks each one with a Tax Free icon.
  • At the register: spend 5,000円 or more before tax at one store on one day, show your passport, and pay the price with tax included. Ask how the refund will be paid.
  • At the airport: within 90 days of the purchase, have customs confirm the goods before you check in your bags. The refund comes after that.
  • On the shelf: open gamekataban.com/?scan=1, scan the barcode on the box, and check the Japanese title and product code.

Can you shop tax-free at BOOKOFF?

Yes, at the stores that take part. BOOKOFF runs an English tax-free site with a store list for each region of Japan, from Hokkaido and Tohoku to Kyushu and Okinawa. Each store in the list is shown with icons, and the Tax Free icon is the one to look for.

Games are eligible. BOOKOFF lists “CD/DVD/Blu-ray/GAME” among the items you can buy tax-free, and the Japan Tourism Agency excludes only gold and platinum bullion and goods that are already exempt from consumption tax.

How does tax-free shopping work at BOOKOFF since 1 November 2026?

Japan switched to the Refund Method for purchases made on or after 1 November 2026. The Tourism Agency explains it this way: you buy at the tax-inclusive price, and the amount equal to the consumption tax is refunded after customs confirms at the airport or seaport that the goods are leaving Japan.

BOOKOFF’s English tax-free page, checked on 9 November 2026, still lists its conditions but does not describe the new procedure or how BOOKOFF pays refunds. Here is what both BOOKOFF and the Tourism Agency state:

  • Minimum spend: 5,000円 or more before tax. The Tourism Agency adds that this means per day, at the same store.
  • Passport: show it when you buy tax-free.

And here is what the Tourism Agency says applies at every tax-free shop:

  • You pay the tax first. The price at the register includes tax.
  • Only what you can carry out. You can buy only as many items as you can take out of Japan yourself when you leave.
  • The refund is paid by the shop or by a refund service provider it uses, not by the government. Possible methods include bank transfer, a refund to a credit card, payment through an app or cash at the airport or seaport, and they differ by shop. The shop explains how to register for the refund when you buy.

BOOKOFF’s page also says a designated handling fee is charged for tax-free purchases and that it may differ from store to store. It does not give an amount, and we found no second source for it, so ask at the register before you pay.

What do you do at the airport?

Customs has to confirm that you are taking the goods out of Japan, or there is no refund. According to the Tourism Agency:

  • Within 90 days of the purchase date, present your passport at a tax-free procedure terminal at the airport or seaport when you leave.
  • Before you check in your bags. The terminals are in the international departure lobby. Once your suitcase has gone to the airline, you cannot do the procedure, and the bag will not be handed back for it.
  • Keep every item on the receipt. Customs checks per receipt. If one item from a receipt is missing, nothing on that receipt is refunded.
  • Connecting flights: do it at the last airport you leave Japan from.

Our guide to the Refund Method covers the airport steps in more detail, including the green and red results and Visit Japan Web.

How do you check a game with Kataban in the store?

  1. Open the scanner. gamekataban.com/?scan=1 opens the barcode scanner straight away.
  2. Scan the box, not the price sticker. Point the camera at the JAN barcode printed on the back of a disc case or a cartridge box. If the camera will not read it, type the 13 digits under the barcode.
  3. Check the result. Kataban shows the Japanese title and the product code (型番, kataban) printed on the cartridge or disc. Compare both with what you are holding. If the barcode is not in Kataban’s catalogue, Kataban checks it against Rakuten’s listings and labels any possible matches as uncertain, so compare the title with the box before you buy.

A bare cartridge with no box has no barcode. Type the product code from its label, or search the English title. Results also show a hint headed “On the shelf · kana order”, because Japanese shops file games by the reading of the title. For disc-era games, which are often shelved by genre, tap “Find by genre & condition guide” to see the Japanese shelf signs next to the English genre names.

What if the staff do not speak English?

Tap “Show to shop staff” on a result. Kataban shows the Japanese title and product code with a question in Japanese, and the staff member taps an answer that appears in Japanese and English. Two of the questions are for the register: “Tax-free?” and “Refund how?”. Ask the second one before you leave the register, because the refund method is set up through the shop, not at the airport. The other questions cover stock, items in the glass case, the box and manual, testing a game and other stores.

Before the trip, you can add games to My List and use “Print / Save PDF” to make a sheet: the left side tells you where to look, and the right side is the part to show the staff.

How is BOOKOFF different from Mandarake?

Mandarake has published how it handles the Refund Method: all its stores take part, and you register with JP Refund before leaving Japan. BOOKOFF had not published those details when we checked. If you are also visiting Mandarake, see our guide to Mandarake’s stores and tax-free shopping. For buying from Japan online instead, see How to Buy Japanese Retro Games Without Reading Japanese.
