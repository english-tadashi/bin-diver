Dynamite Headdy on Mega Drive: Treasure’s 1994 Puppet Action Game, Its Code G-4117, and Why It Never Got a Second Part

2026-11-17

  • Mega Drive (Genesis)
  • Platformer
  • 1990s
  • Sega

Dynamite Headdy — ダイナマイトヘッディー — is a side-scrolling action game that Sega released in Japan on 5 August 1994 for the Mega Drive, product code G-4117, at 6,800円 before tax. It was made by Treasure, the studio behind Gunstar Heroes. You play Headdy, a puppet who fights by throwing his own head — and swaps it for seventeen other heads along the way.

This is the fifth entry in a series about Mega Drive games that never became a series. There was never a Dynamite Headdy 2. The same game came out the same day on the Game Gear, later on a Brazilian Master System, and since then in a long list of compilations and downloads — all of them the 1994 game, none of them a second part.

  • Japanese title: ダイナマイトヘッディー, published by Sega, developed by Treasure
  • Mega Drive cartridge: 5 August 1994 · 6,800円 before tax · G-4117 — the Media Arts Database and Sega’s own Mega Drive software list agree on the date and the price, and Sega’s list gives the code
  • Game Gear cartridge: the same day · 3,800円 · G-3355, by Sega’s Game Gear list
  • Western releases: September (North America) and October (Europe) 1994, made harder and with most dialogue cut
  • No sequel: a 32X version was planned and cancelled; every later release is the same game
  • Buying it: the Japanese title at Suruga-ya, and the title plus メガドライブ on Buyee

What is Dynamite Headdy, and how does it play?

Both Wikipedias describe the same idea: Headdy throws his head in any direction, and it comes back to him like a boomerang. English Wikipedia adds that he uses it to grab hooks and pull himself up, that 17 power-up heads change what it does, and that falling into a pit bounces him back up instead of killing him. The world is a puppet show; the villain, King Dark Demon, turns the puppets he likes into his minions and throws the rest away. Japanese Wikipedia counts the stages as “scenes”, 30 in all.

English Wikipedia says Treasure split into four teams after Gunstar Heroes, one of them for this game, which started in August 1993 as an original idea of Koichi Kimura’s, with Treasure’s president Masato Maegawa as lead programmer and Western animation and Terry Gilliam as visual influences. Famicom Tsūshin gave it 28 out of 40, by Japanese Wikipedia, and Mega Drive Fan readers 24.4 out of 30. These are the sources’ descriptions; we have not played through the game to check them.

Which version is which: Mega Drive, Game Gear or Western?

The Japanese Mega Drive cartridge is G-4117: Sega’s list gives 8月 5日｜ダイナマイトヘッディー｜アクション｜6,800円｜G-4117｜16Ｍ, and the Media Arts Database row M750876 the same date and price. The Game Gear version is G-3355, released the same day at 3,800円; Japanese Wikipedia says it was developed by Minato Giken under Treasure’s supervision, and English Wikipedia calls it an 8-bit port. On the shelf the two carry the same title, so the system name is what tells them apart.

The Western Mega Drive and Genesis versions are not the same game in every detail. English Wikipedia says Sega of America asked for it to be harder, so that renters would not finish it too quickly, and the North American release became about twice as difficult, with most of the dialogue removed and some character and boss sprites changed. On Suruga-ya those copies are filed as GENISIS(北米)版 and MEGADRIVE(EU)版; on eBay, “PAL” or “Genesis” means one of them.

Why was there never a Dynamite Headdy 2?

We could not find a reason given anywhere, so we will not offer one. English Wikipedia mentions one more version that did not happen — a 32X conversion, planned and cancelled — and neither Wikipedia mentions a sequel. The Media Arts Database has the Mega Drive and Game Gear cartridges and the Wii download, all under the same name. Treasure’s other games of the time, such as Gunstar Heroes and Alien Soldier, were separate projects by other teams, not continuations of this one.

Has it been re-released?

Many times, always as the same game. Japanese Wikipedia lists a Master System port sold only in Brazil in 1995; the PlayStation 2 compilation セガエイジス2500シリーズ Vol.25 ガンスターヒーローズ 〜トレジャーボックス〜 (2006), which English Wikipedia says includes every version from all three systems; the Wii Virtual Console from 24 July 2007; Steam in 2012; and the Mega Drive Mini in 2019. English Wikipedia adds Sonic’s Ultimate Genesis Collection, Sega Genesis Classics (2018) and Nintendo Classics (2021). Ports and reissues of the same game do not count as a second part in this series.
