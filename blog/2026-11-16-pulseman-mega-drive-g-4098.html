Pulseman on Mega Drive: Game Freak’s 1994 Action Game, Its Code G-4098, and Why It Never Got a Second Part

2026-11-16

  • Mega Drive (Genesis)
  • Platformer
  • 1990s
  • Sega

Pulseman — パルスマン — is a side-scrolling action game that Sega released in Japan on 22 July 1994 for the Mega Drive, product code G-4098, at 7,800円 before tax. It was made by Game Freak, the studio that later created Pokémon. You play Pulseman, half human and half computer life-form, who can charge himself with electricity and shoot through the stage as a ball of energy.

This is the fourth entry in a series about Mega Drive games that never became a series. There was never a Pulseman 2. What did carry on is a move: Pulseman’s electric charge, ボルテッカー (Volteccer), reappeared as a Pikachu attack in Game Freak’s next hit. A borrowed move is not a sequel, and we explain why below.

  • Japanese title: パルスマン, published by Sega, developed by Game Freak
  • Mega Drive cartridge: 22 July 1994 · 7,800円 before tax · G-4098 — the Media Arts Database and Sega’s own Mega Drive software list agree on the date and the price, and Sega’s list gives the code
  • Outside Japan: by English Wikipedia, North America got it in 1995 through the Sega Channel service
  • No sequel: its Volteccer and some names turned up in Pokémon, but nothing continued the game
  • Re-released: Wii Virtual Console (Japan 2007, North America and Europe 2009) and Nintendo Classics (2023)
  • Buying it: the Japanese title at Suruga-ya and on Buyee — where most listings are its soundtrack records

What is Pulseman, and how does it play?

Japanese Wikipedia describes a hero who can move between the real world and the inside of computers. On foot he punches, slides and kicks; once charged up he can fire a slash arrow or turn into the Volteccer, a ball of electricity that bounces off walls in straight lines and rides along power cables. Water is his weakness. There are eight stages, from a television station in Japan to the villain’s base in a Japanese game centre, and the first six can be chosen in two groups of three. English Wikipedia gives the story: Pulseman is the son of a scientist and the artificial life-form he fell in love with, and he fights his own father, who has become the evil Doc Waruyama.

Both Wikipedias say it was announced as Spark. English Wikipedia says it was directed by Ken Sugimori; Japanese Wikipedia credits direction and design to Sugimori and Satoshi Tajiri, and programming and music to Junichi Masuda — names that would all appear on Pokémon. Japanese Wikipedia also says Tajiri and Sugimori built the speed of light into the hero as a rival to Sonic the Hedgehog, faster than sound. Famicom Tsūshin gave it 24 out of 40, by Japanese Wikipedia; both Wikipedias give 20.9 out of 30 from Mega Drive Fan readers. These are the sources’ descriptions; we have not played through the game to check them.

Is Pokémon a continuation of Pulseman?

No. Japanese Wikipedia lists what passed from one to the other: ボルテッカー became a move used by Pichu, Pikachu and Raichu; a name like パルスワン and the computer サント・アンヌ reappear; Pikachu’s Final Smash in Super Smash Bros. Brawl (2008) uses an effect based on this game’s; and the idea of storing Pokémon inside a PC recalls Pulseman moving in and out of computers. English Wikipedia adds that much of the staff went on to work on Pokémon. A move, a few names and the people who made it carried on; the game itself, its hero and its world did not. For this series that is reuse, not a second part.

Why was there never a Pulseman 2?

We could not find a reason given anywhere, so we will not offer one. The Media Arts Database has one Mega Drive Pulseman row, and neither Wikipedia mentions a sequel. Game Freak went on to Pokémon, but neither source connects that to the absence of a Pulseman 2, and we do not either.

Has it been re-released?

Yes, as a download, never as a new cartridge. English Wikipedia says it came to the Wii Virtual Console in Japan in 2007 and in North America and Europe in 2009, and to Nintendo Classics on 18 April 2023; Sega’s own Mega Drive list links the Pulseman row to the Virtual Console site. Japanese Wikipedia notes one difference: because of the so-called Pokémon Shock, the Pokémon anime episode that caused seizures, the Virtual Console version has a darker screen than the original. It is not on the Mega Drive Mini or the Genesis Mini. These versions are the 1994 game, so for this series they do not count as a second part — and none of them is something you can buy second-hand.

Why search Kataban for this one?

Because the code is no help in the shops — at Suruga-ya and on Buyee G-4098 and G4098 return other products — and the title search mixes in records and books. On Kataban, G-4098 returns this cartridge alone, and Pulseman or パルスマン return two rows: this cartridge and the 2007 Wii download, marked as having no disc or cartridge. The Japanese title is next to each to copy into a shop. In second-hand game shops in Japan, such as Book-Off, games are often shelved in Japanese syllabary order, and this one is under ハ.

A hero made of electricity, a move that outlived him, and a team that went on to something bigger: this cartridge was never followed by a second one. If you want the original, the code is G-4098, and the title to search is パルスマン.
