Super Arabian on Famicom: Sunsoft’s First Home Game, and Its Product Code SS1-4500

2026-11-26

  • Famicom (NES)
  • Platformer
  • 1980s
  • Sunsoft
  • Japan Only

Super Arabian (スーパーアラビアン) is a Sunsoft action game, released on the Famicom in Japan in July 1985 — and, in Sunsoft’s own words, the company’s first home game. You play Arabian, a prince who sets out to rescue a kidnapped princess, kicking enemies out of the way and collecting magic jars. Knock out several enemies at once and he becomes, for a while, the invincible Super Arabian. The game began as Arabian, a 1983 arcade game from Sun Electronics.

On 10 September 2026, Sun Corporation, which publishes games under the Sunsoft brand, announced SUNSOFT is Back! Retro Game Selection 2 for Nintendo Switch, PlayStation 5 and Steam, due in Japan on 17 December 2026 with Super Arabian among its four Famicom games. The announcement introduces it with one line: 「1985年に発売されたSUNSOFT初の家庭用ゲーム」 — “Sunsoft’s first home game, released in 1985”. That is the easy way to play it. This guide is about the other way: owning the 1985 cartridge, and finding it by its product code, SS1-4500.

  • Japanese title: スーパーアラビアン (Super Arabian), published by Sunsoft (サンソフト), the game brand of Sun Corporation (サン電子)
  • Famicom: 25 July 1985 in most listings (FamiWiki says 26 July) · product code SS1-4500
  • List price: 4,500円 in three Japanese listings, ¥4,800 on FamiWiki — the 4500 in the code matches the first
  • Sunsoft’s first: Sunsoft’s 2026 press release calls it its first home game; the Japanese Wikipedia article calls it Sunsoft’s first Famicom release
  • Arcade original: Arabian, 1983, released in North America by Atari; the Famicom version came out only in Japan
  • New release: Retro Game Selection 2, 17 December 2026 in Japan (Switch, PS5, Steam) — Super Arabian is also sold on its own as a download
  • Find it by code: SS1-4500 on Kataban returns this one cartridge; the English name Super Arabian finds it too (1 result, rechecked 12 October 2026)

What is Super Arabian?

Sunsoft’s 2026 press release describes it as an action game full of the atmosphere of the Showa era, in which Arabian sets out to rescue a kidnapped princess: 「敵をなぎ倒しながら、すべての魔法の壺を集めよう。敵をまとめて倒すと無敵のスーパーアラビアンになれるぞ。」 — “Knock down the enemies and collect all the magic jars. Take out enemies together, and you become the invincible Super Arabian!” The English Steam page puts it as “Take out multiple enemies at once to transform into the invincible Super Arabian!”

The Japanese listings fill in the rest. According to the Japanese Wikipedia article, each of the four stages has six jars, and collecting them all clears the stage. Enemies go down in one hit, but touching one costs you a life, and you start with three. Arabian carries a sword, but fights with his feet: kick an enemy and, as Pikopiko Daihyakka describes it, it flies sideways and can take other enemies down with it. Take out three or more at once and you become Super Arabian. There is no ending; after stage 4 the game starts over. The princess is Leia (レイア) in both gamecatalog.net and Pikopiko Daihyakka. The Japanese Wikipedia article is flagged for lacking sources, but Pikopiko gives the same three-enemy rule.

The same article lists what changed from the arcade game: the letters on the jars no longer only spell ARABIAN, the music is different, the backgrounds are in colour, the three-enemy kick that makes you invincible is new, and in stage 4 touching the princess no longer ends the stage — you have to collect every jar.

Why is Super Arabian called Sunsoft’s first home game?

Because Sunsoft says so, and the listings agree. The 2026 press release and the collection’s official Japanese site both open its entry with 「1985年に発売されたSUNSOFT初の家庭用ゲーム」, and the English Steam page with “Sunsoft’s first home console game, released in 1985.” The Japanese Wikipedia article calls it 「サンソフトのファミコン参入第1弾ソフト」 — the first game Sunsoft released when it entered the Famicom market. FamiWiki says “It is the first game Sunsoft released for the system”, and arcade-history that it “was the first home video game made by Sunsoft.”

The manual counts it as number one. The front cover of the manual, which we checked in a scan at the Internet Archive, is printed SERIES 1 in its top-left corner. gamecatalog.net files the game as Sunsoft’s “SERIES1”, and the product code starts SS1.

One Sunsoft source gets the date wrong. A 2022 press release from Sun Corporation says Sunsoft began developing arcade games in the late 1970s and 「80年代後半には初のファミコン用タイトル「スーパーアラビアン」を発売開始」 — that it started selling its first Famicom title, Super Arabian, in the late 1980s. Sunsoft’s own 2026 release, and every listing we checked, give 1985.

Did Super Arabian ever come out on the NES?

No. The arcade original did travel: the English Wikipedia article says Arabian was released in North America in May 1983 by Atari, and in Japan in June 1983, and that a separate version for home computers was released as Tales of the Arabian Nights. But the same article says the Famicom version “was developed and released by Sunsoft only in Japan as Super Arabian.” So there is no NES cartridge to look for; the only one is the Japanese Famicom cartridge.

What is Retro Game Selection 2, and is it the same as the cartridge?

It is the Famicom game — but not a cartridge. SUNSOFT is Back! Retro Game Selection 2 collects four early Sunsoft Famicom games: Super Arabian, Ikki, Atlantis no Nazo (sold on Steam as The Mystery of Atlantis) and Maharaja. It adds save and rewind features and a gallery of original artwork and flyers. Sunsoft’s Japanese site gives the release date as Thursday, 17 December 2026, on Nintendo Switch, PlayStation 5 and PC (Steam), and lists English among the supported languages.

In Japan the collection is 5,478円 as a package and 4,378円 as a download (2,970円 on Steam), with a 10,890円 Collector’s Box. Super Arabian is also sold on its own as a download, at 1,540円 (1,320円 on Steam). One difference from the cartridge: the press release lists that download as one player (プレイ人数：1人), while the cartridge let two players take turns, according to gamecatalog.net (同時プレイ 2人交互). Outside Japan, the dates are not set yet: on 13 September 2026, Steam said “To be announced”.

The collection is not the first re-release. The English Wikipedia article lists Memorial Series Sunsoft Vol. 1 for PlayStation (2001), which paired it with Ikki, and the Sunsoft Collection 1 cartridge for the Evercade (2023); the Japanese article adds two Windows compilations (2001 and 2004). The arcade original, Arabian, came to PlayStation 4 and Switch in 2020 as part of Hamster’s Arcade Archives. If you want to play Super Arabian, any of those will do. If you want to own the original, that is the 1985 Famicom cartridge — and a Famicom cartridge needs a Famicom, since its 60-pin board does not fit an NES without an adapter.

What is the product code, and where is it printed?

The Famicom cartridge is SS1-4500. Four sources give it: arcade-history (“Game ID: SS1-4500”), the Japanese game catalog gamecatalog.net, Pikopiko Daihyakka, which writes it without the hyphen as SS14500, and satakore, a site that gathers auction listings. The Internet Archive’s scan of the manual is also filed under SS1-4500.

Unlike Ikki and Atlantis no Nazo, we have not been able to check where the code is printed. We have not found a scan of the back of the box, and the manual’s front and back covers carry no code. FamiWiki, which gives codes for Sunsoft’s other early games, has no code field on its Super Arabian page.

The code is not in the Agency for Cultural Affairs Media Arts Database, which Kataban’s catalog is built on. We added it on 13 September 2026 from the sources above, so the catalog card for Super Arabian now shows it.

Was it 4,500 or 4,800 yen?

The sources do not agree, and we have not seen a price printed on the box or in a period advertisement to settle it.

  • 4,500円, 25 July 1985: Pikopiko Daihyakka, gamecatalog.net and the Media Arts Database (4500円 (税抜), before tax). Both Wikipedia articles and arcade-history give the same date
  • ¥4800, 26 July 1985: FamiWiki

The product code sides with the first group: the 4500 in SS1-4500 matches 4,500円, just as Ikki’s SS3-4900 matches its 4,900円. That is a pattern, not proof. The question will not help you find a copy, either, since the code is the same either way.
