Machi on Sega Saturn: Chunsoft’s 1998 Sound Novel, Its Code T-34001G, and Why 428 Was Not Made as Its Sequel

2026-11-11

  • Sega Saturn
  • Adventure
  • 1990s
  • Chunsoft

Machi — 街, “City”, sold on the Saturn as サウンドノベル 街 (“Sound Novel Machi”) — is a text adventure that Chunsoft released in Japan on 22 January 1998 for the Sega Saturn, product code T-34001G, at 5,800円 before tax, on two discs. It follows eight people through five days in Shibuya, and what one of them does can wreck another’s story.

This is the sixth entry in a series about Sega Saturn games that never became a series. Machi was ported and re-released for a decade, and ten years later Chunsoft made another game set in Shibuya, 428: Shibuya Scramble. But 428 was deliberately not made as Machi 2, by its director’s own account, and a planned sequel ended up as a television drama instead.

  • Japanese title: 街 (box: サウンドノベル 街 -machi-), published by Chunsoft; the later ports add the subtitle 〜運命の交差点〜
  • Sega Saturn CD: 22 January 1998 · 5,800円 before tax · T-34001G · two discs
  • Later versions: PlayStation (1999, budget reissue 2002) and PSP (2006, with two extra scenarios; budget reissue 2007; download 2012) — all the same game
  • No sequel: a proposed sequel was cancelled; 428 (2008) shares the setting, and its director says it was made as something new rather than as a sequel
  • Watch out for: the Saturn demo disc and the PlayStation and PSP versions, which share the name
  • Buying it: the code at Suruga-ya, and the Saturn title サウンドノベル 街 plus セガサターン on Buyee

What is Machi, and how does it play?

You read it. Like Chunsoft’s earlier sound novels, which Japanese Wikipedia names as 弟切草 (1992) and かまいたちの夜 (1994), it is mostly text with pictures and sound, and you choose what the characters do. Both Wikipedias describe eight main characters — among them a detective, an actor, a former gangster and a university student — and Japanese Wikipedia explains the trick: they mostly never meet, but a choice in one story can send another to a bad ending, so you jump between them, which the game calls “zapping”, to find out who caused what.

Japanese Wikipedia adds that it is told with photographs of real actors, that the story runs from 11 to 15 October, and that it won the scenario award at the third CESA Awards. English Wikipedia says it sold 164,866 copies in Japan and that Famitsu scored it 33 out of 40; those two figures come from that one article. These are the sources’ descriptions; we have not played through the game to check them.

Which version is the Saturn disc?

The Saturn disc is T-34001G. The Media Arts Database has it as row M719412: published on 22 January 1998 by Chunsoft, two computer discs, [5800円(税抜)], titled 街 from the disc label, with サウンドノベル 街 as an alternate title. Sega’s own list of licensed Saturn software gives the same date, price and code. Suruga-ya files it simply as 街.

The other versions are easy to tell once you know the words. Both Wikipedias say it was ported to the PlayStation in 1999 as 街 〜運命の交差点〜; Japanese Wikipedia dates it to 28 January 1999 as part of a サウンドノベル・エボリューション line, with a budget reissue on 4 April 2002, and the Media Arts Database has PlayStation rows for 1999 and 2002. Suruga-ya lists these as サウンドノベル・エボリューション3 and PSoneBooks. The PSP version, 街 〜運命の交差点〜 特別篇, came out on 27 April 2006 with two extra scenarios, and again as SEGA THE BEST on 30 August 2007, by Japanese Wikipedia; English Wikipedia also dates the PSP version to 2006, and the Media Arts Database has PSP rows for 2006 and 2007.

So: 運命の交差点, PlayStation, SLPS, PSone Books, 特別篇, PSP or UMD in a listing means a later version. And watch for the free demo: Suruga-ya lists 街 [体験版] (“trial version”) and 街[サンプル版] (“sample”), and on Buyee and eBay the demo appears often, alone or in a three-disc promotional set, お楽しみ3シロ！DISC, with Sonic R and Shining Force III. 体験版, サンプル, 非売品 (“not for sale”), “trial” or “demo” means it is not the game.

How is it related to 428: Shibuya Scramble?

Closely, by every account — but not as a sequel, by its makers’ account. Chunsoft’s 428 came out in 2008, set in Shibuya and told, like Machi, with live-action photographs and several main characters. Japanese Wikipedia says the two have much in common and that, while 428 has no direct connection to Machi’s story, a few lines hint that it takes place ten years later. It quotes 428’s director, Jiro Ishii (イシイジロウ): Shibuya was chosen partly to please Machi’s fans, and it was not made as a sequel to Machi because Machi had not been a commercial success, so they took its best parts and offered something new and original.

English Wikipedia puts it the same way from the other side: 428 shares many story and gameplay elements with Machi, “Although Chunsoft does not openly state 428 is a sequel”, it contains numerous references to it. For this series, a game that borrows Machi’s setting and system and is presented by its makers as something new is not a second Machi, and we have kept the sources’ words rather than replace them with ours.

Why was there never a Machi 2?

One was proposed. English Wikipedia says a proposed sequel was cancelled, and that a chapter of it was adapted as a 1998 television miniseries, 透明少女エア (Invisible Girl Ea); that detail is from English Wikipedia alone. Ishii’s explanation of 428 — that Machi had not sold well enough to justify a sequel — is the only reason we found given anywhere, and it comes from Japanese Wikipedia’s article on 428.

Has it been re-released?

Many times, but never on the Saturn again. Besides the PlayStation and PSP versions above, Japanese Wikipedia says a mobile-phone version was released by Dwango in 2005, and that the PSP version went on sale as a download on the PlayStation Store on 27 March 2012, playable on PS Vita; the Media Arts Database has a PSP row for 2012. Sega’s Saturn list has no link on the Machi row. All of these are the same game, ported, not new ones. We found no English release of Machi itself.

Why search Kataban for this one?

Because a one-character title is hard to search for anywhere, and Kataban keeps the versions apart. T-34001G returns the Saturn disc, with the Japanese title next to it to copy into a shop. 街 運命の交差点 returns all six entries — the Saturn disc, the two PlayStation releases and the three PSP ones — each with its platform, and 428 finds the game that came after it. In second-hand game shops in Japan, such as Book-Off, games are often shelved in Japanese syllabary order, and this one is under マ.

Eight people, five days, one district of Tokyo, and a sequel that became a TV drama instead: Machi stayed one game, even when Chunsoft went back to Shibuya. If you want it as it first came out, the code is T-34001G, the box says サウンドノベル 街, and it should not say 体験版.
